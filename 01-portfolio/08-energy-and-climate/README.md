# 08 — Energy and Climate

> **Citizen promise:** Lower bills, reliable power, real climate action.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria is closing coal, building renewables and reviving the SEC, but it is still running three transitions in parallel: generation, networks and household electrification. The next move is to **electrify and flex everything**: use the SEC as an investor and developer, remove the friction from household electrification, and manage demand as a resource.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Renewables reached **42.4%** of Victorian generation in 2024–25, above the 40% 2025 target. The state has legislated **65% by 2030 and 95% by 2035**, with **773 MW** of storage commissioned and **2.3 GW** under construction or commissioning. The SEC has more than **1 GW** of committed capacity toward **4.5 GW by 2035**. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Energy and Resources, Climate Action, Environment and State Electricity Commission (Jaclyn Symes).
**Department(s):** Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA).
**Key agencies:** State Electricity Commission (SEC); Essential Services Commission (ESC); VicGrid; Australian Energy Market Operator (AEMO, national); EPA; energy networks; generators and retailers; Solar Victoria.
**Statute / framework:** Electricity Industry Act; Climate Change Act; Renewable Energy and Storage Targets; National Electricity Rules; emissions reduction targets.
**Scale:** Whole-of-state energy system; SEC initial **$1 billion** toward **4.5 GW** of generation and storage by 2035. [A]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Households and businesses pay less for reliable energy.
- **Outcome 2:** The grid is ready for renewables, storage and electrification.
- **Outcome 3:** Victoria meets its 2030 and 2035 targets without sacrificing reliability.
- **Outcome 4:** Households can electrify cheaply and fairly.
- **Outcome 5:** The coal transition is managed for workers and communities.

**What success looks like by 2030:** 65% renewables; enough firming capacity; falling household bills; more rooftop solar and batteries; a managed Yallourn closure; and a SEC with a real development pipeline.

**What failure looks like:** Blackouts, price spikes, a rushed coal exit, stranded gas assets, and households left behind because they cannot afford to electrify.

**What the citizen experiences:** Confusing bills, complex concessions, unreliable connections, long waits for solar and battery installs, and no clear advice on electrification.
**What the frontline experiences:** Network constraints, connection queues, skills shortages, changing rules and a market that is hard to explain.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Network bottlenecks:** Transmission and distribution constraints delay renewables and raise costs.
- **Coal exit risk:** Yallourn is scheduled to close by 30 June 2028; replacement capacity and firming must be ready. [A]
- **Household electrification friction:** Upfront cost, installer quality, connection delays and confusing advice slow uptake.
- **Fragmented programs:** Solar, battery, efficiency and concession programs are delivered separately.
- **Fossil fuel support:** Subsidies and concessions can lock in higher-cost, higher-emission choices.
- **Consumer protections:** Vulnerable households face disconnection and debt.
- **Workforce:** Electricians, engineers and construction workers are in short supply.
- **Social licence:** Renewable projects face local opposition without genuine benefit-sharing.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are network costs, avoided bill savings, health costs of emissions and the cost of a disorderly coal exit. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** The state sets targets and supports projects; the market delivers; households adopt if they can afford it.

**The new frame:** The state is an **investor, market shaper and electrification enabler**. It builds the firming and network capacity, de-risks household upgrades, and manages demand as a resource.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Target plus subsidy** | Keep targets, fund projects, offer rebates | Familiar | Does not fix networks or equity | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. Public investor and enabler** | SEC invests; state coordinates networks, electrification and demand | Faster, fairer | Requires capital and capability | Preferred base |
| **C. Fully public retail** | State sells energy directly | Visible price control | Market complexity and risk | Not recommended as default |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Electrify everything, flex everything.** The cheapest energy is the energy we do not use at peak. Use smart tariffs, batteries, hot water, EV charging and industrial demand response to flatten the peak, and fund household electrification so that everyone can participate.

**Supporting moves:**

- **SEC as developer/investor:** co-invest in renewables, storage and grid infrastructure, with a public return.
- **One-stop electrification service:** assessment, finance, installer, connection and quality assurance in one place.
- **Social licence deals:** community benefit-sharing, local ownership and First Peoples partnerships.
- **Coal transition authority:** workers, communities and land, with a funded plan.
- **Energy efficiency as infrastructure:** insulation, draught-proofing, efficient appliances and heat pumps.
- **Network investment discipline:** use non-network alternatives and demand response before building poles and wires.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate household energy programs | DEECA/Solar Victoria | One front door | Admin cost and confusion | [D] |
| Remove fossil fuel subsidies and concessions that slow electrification | DEECA/DTF | Targeted support for affected households | Fiscal and emissions | [D] |
| Stop funding low-value energy programs without evidence | DEECA | Reallocate to electrification | Program savings | [D] |
| Reduce network gold-plating through demand response | Networks/AER | Customer bill savings | Network capex | [D] |
| Sunset outdated gas connection incentives | DEECA | Targeted transition support | Emissions and cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Ending fossil fuel support and changing gas connections will be contested by industry and some households. It requires a fair transition package.
**What we will not cut:** Reliability; support for vulnerable households; coal transition support; renewable investment.

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **invest, coordinate networks, run consumer programs and manage the coal transition**.

**The new deal:** The household gets lower bills and a clear path to electrify. The region gets investment and jobs. The state gets reliability, emissions reduction and a public return.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | National energy framework and funding | Faster transition | State acts alone |
| AEMO / AER | Market operation and rules | Reliable system | Price spikes |
| Networks | Investment and connections | Regulated returns | Bottlenecks |
| Generators / retailers | Supply and competition | Market certainty | Price volatility |
| Unions / workers | Transition cooperation | Good jobs, training | Industrial action |
| Communities / First Peoples | Social licence | Benefit-sharing, ownership | Project delays |
| Households | Demand flexibility | Lower bills | Peak costs |
| Industry | Electrification and demand response | Competitiveness | Higher costs |

**The convening mechanism:** A Energy Transition Board with DEECA, SEC, AEMO, networks, industry, unions, communities and First Peoples.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of bills, reliability, renewables, storage, emissions and SEC investment.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true cost of household electrification by dwelling type.
- The network investment needed for 65% and 95% renewables.
- The best model for SEC investment and return.
- The social licence conditions for renewable projects.
- The coal transition costs for workers and communities.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A household electrification cost model.
- A network and firming adequacy review.
- A SEC investment framework.
- A coal transition plan.
