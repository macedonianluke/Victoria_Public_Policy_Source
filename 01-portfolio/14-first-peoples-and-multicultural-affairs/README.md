# 14 — First Peoples and Multicultural Affairs

> **Citizen promise:** Self-determination, truth and belonging.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria has made historic Treaty and truth-telling progress, but the machinery of government has not yet changed how it shares power, data and money. The next move is to make **Treaty and self-determination the operating model**, not a portfolio: fund Aboriginal-led outcomes, respect data sovereignty, and build a multicultural system that treats language, culture and racism as design factors.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria's first **Statewide Treaty** was signed on **13 November 2025** and commenced on **12 December 2025**. From **1 May 2026**, the First Peoples' Assembly evolved into **Gellung Warl**, a permanent representative body with accountability and truth-telling functions. Yoorrook's final reports contain **148 recommendations**, including **100 in Yoorrook for Transformation**; the 2026–27 Budget allocated **$6 million over two years** to begin implementation. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for First Peoples, Education, Skills and Training, Women and Gender Equality (Gabrielle Williams); Minister for Multicultural Affairs, Police, Emergency Services and Crime Prevention (Paul Edbrooke).
**Department(s):** Department of Premier and Cabinet (DPC) — First Peoples-State Relations; Department of Families, Fairness and Housing (DFFH); Department of Justice and Community Safety (DJCS); Department of Health (DH).
**Key agencies:** Gellung Warl; Treaty Authority; Yoorrook Justice Commission (transition); Registered Aboriginal Parties; Traditional Owner groups; Aboriginal community-controlled organisations; Victorian Multicultural Commission (VMC); multicultural service providers.
**Statute / framework:** Treaty Negotiation Framework; Advancing the Treaty Process with Aboriginal Victorians Act; Aboriginal Heritage Act; Multicultural Victoria Act; Charter of Human Rights; Closing the Gap.
**Scale:** Whole-of-government obligations, with dedicated funding and a growing Aboriginal community-controlled sector. [A]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Treaty commitments are implemented, not just signed.
- **Outcome 2:** Aboriginal Victorians have decision-making power over the policies and funding that affect them.
- **Outcome 3:** Truth-telling leads to practical change in justice, child protection, health, housing and education.
- **Outcome 4:** Multicultural communities have equitable access, language support and protection from racism.
- **Outcome 5:** Victoria is a place where all people belong.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Closing the Gap targets improve; Treaty institutions exercise real power; Aboriginal-led services are funded at scale; language services are accessible; and reported racism falls.

**What failure looks like:** Another apology, another strategy, and the same outcomes gap.

**What the citizen experiences:** A system that still asks Aboriginal people to fit into programs designed without them. Multicultural communities navigate English-only information, culturally unsafe services and racism.
**What the frontline experiences:** Contracting and reporting requirements that undermine self-determination; short funding cycles; a gap between Treaty rhetoric and budget decisions.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Implementation gap:** Treaty and Yoorrook recommendations are not yet matched with a whole-of-government implementation budget and accountability.
- **Fragmented Aboriginal affairs:** Multiple departments and agencies fund overlapping programs without a single outcome framework.
- **Short-term contracts:** Aboriginal community-controlled organisations lack the stability to build capability.
- **Data sovereignty:** Data about Aboriginal people is collected, held and used without adequate control or benefit.
- **Cultural safety:** Mainstream services are not consistently safe or accessible.
- **Language services:** Fragmented, underfunded and hard to navigate.
- **Racism:** Under-reported and inconsistently responded to.
- **Multicultural fragmentation:** Many small programs, weak evaluation and limited community control.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest costs are poor outcomes in justice, child protection, health, housing and education, plus the administrative burden of fragmented funding. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Aboriginal and multicultural affairs are special programs added to mainstream portfolios.

**The new frame:** Treaty and self-determination are a **governance reset**. Aboriginal Victorians decide; government funds, supports and is accountable. Multicultural policy is **mainstream policy designed for diversity**, not a separate add-on.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Program expansion** | More targeted programs and grants | Visible | Does not shift power | Not sufficient |
| **B. Self-determined funding** | Aboriginal-led outcomes, pooled funding, data sovereignty | Real power and better outcomes | Requires trust and capability | Preferred base |
| **C. Treaty-based co-governance** | Shared decision-making across portfolios | Structural change | Long negotiation and uncertainty | Long-term direction |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Fund outcomes, not programs, and let Aboriginal organisations decide how to deliver them.** A single outcome agreement per community or region, with multi-year funding and data control, replaces the grant maze.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Treaty implementation unit:** a whole-of-government unit with budget authority and reporting to Cabinet.
- **Yoorrook response:** a public, costed response to all 100 transformation recommendations.
- **Data sovereignty:** Aboriginal-controlled data agreements, collection and reporting.
- **Cultural safety standards:** across health, justice, child protection, housing and education.
- **Language services guarantee:** one front door, interpreters and translated information.
- **Anti-racism strategy:** reporting, education and enforcement.
- **Multicultural community hubs:** local, community-led access to services.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate Aboriginal affairs units across departments | DPC/departments | One accountability framework | Admin cost | [D] |
| Replace short-term grants with multi-year outcome agreements | DPC/departments | Stable Aboriginal-led delivery | Admin and service churn | [D] |
| Sunset culturally unsafe or ineffective programs | Departments | Reallocate to Aboriginal-led services | Program savings | [D] |
| Consolidate fragmented language and multicultural programs | VMC/DFFH | One front door | Admin cost | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate reporting on Aboriginal outcomes | Departments | Shared data and reporting | Provider time | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Shifting power and funding to Aboriginal organisations will be resisted by departments and some providers. It is the point of Treaty.
**What we will not cut:** Treaty institutions; Aboriginal-led services; language services; anti-racism.

**The new capability:** The public service must be able to **share power, fund outcomes, respect data sovereignty and design for cultural safety**.

**The new deal:** Aboriginal Victorians get decision-making power. Multicultural communities get access and respect. The state gets better outcomes and a more legitimate relationship.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Closing the Gap, NDIS, health, education | Better outcomes | State fills gaps |
| Gellung Warl / Treaty Authority | Representation and oversight | Power, resources, accountability | Treaty stalled |
| Traditional Owners / RAPs | Cultural authority | Land, water, self-determination | Conflict |
| Aboriginal community organisations | Delivery and trust | Multi-year funding | Service gaps |
| Multicultural communities | Engagement | Access and anti-racism | Exclusion |
| Local government | Local delivery | Partnership and funding | Fragmentation |
| Mainstream services | Cultural safety | Better outcomes | Harm and complaints |
| All Victorians | Recognition and respect | A fairer state | Division |

**The convening mechanism:** A Treaty Implementation Cabinet Committee and a First Peoples and Multicultural Advisory Council.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of Closing the Gap, Treaty implementation, cultural safety, language access and racism.

---

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **All portfolios** — Treaty and self-determination change how policy is made, not just what is funded.
- **Justice, police and community safety** — over-representation and healing.
- **Health and mental health** — culturally safe care.
- **Housing and planning** — Aboriginal housing and land.
- **Education and early childhood** — cultural safety and outcomes.
- **Local government** — local partnerships and Reconciliation Action Plans.
- **Fiscal and public sector** — pooled funding and outcome agreements.

**The shared asset:** A whole-of-government Treaty and Closing the Gap accountability framework.

## 11. Open questions and sources

- The current share of Aboriginal-controlled funding.

- The language service demand and gaps.

- A Treaty implementation costing.
- An Aboriginal funding audit.
- A data sovereignty pilot.
