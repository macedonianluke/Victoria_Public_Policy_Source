# 01 — Fiscal and Public Sector

> **Citizen promise:** Your taxes buy outcomes, not org charts.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria does not need another efficiency review. It needs an **outcome budget**, a **single back office**, a **sunset default** and a **delivery office with the power to stop things**. The savings are real, but they are only durable if the capability is rebuilt at the same time.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Net debt is forecast at **$173.1 billion** at 30 June 2027, with **$8.9 billion** of interest expense and **$20.0 billion** of infrastructure investment. The operating balance is a **$655 million surplus**, but the cash deficit is **$7.7 billion**. [A/B]

**Minister(s):** Treasurer and Minister for Industrial Relations (Colin Brooks); Minister for Finance and Minister for Government Services (Enver Erdogan).
**Department(s):** Department of Treasury and Finance (DTF); Department of Government Services (DGS); Department of Premier and Cabinet (DPC); Victorian Public Sector Commission (VPSC).
**Key agencies:** Victorian Auditor-General's Office (VAGO); Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission (IBAC); Victorian Ombudsman; Essential Services Commission (ESC); Treasury Corporation of Victoria; Victorian Funds Management Corporation; Victorian Managed Insurance Authority; WorkSafe; TAC.
**Statute / framework:** Financial Management Act, Appropriation Acts, Public Administration Act, Audit Act, Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission Act, Freedom of Information Act.
**Scale:** 10 departments; about 306 listed departments, agencies and services; a public service of more than 300,000 people across the broader public sector. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

The state's fiscal job is not to minimise spending. It is to **convert revenue into outcomes with the least waste, the least risk and the most trust**.

- **Outcome 1:** A sustainable fiscal trajectory: debt stabilises and interest costs stop crowding out services.
- **Outcome 2:** Every dollar of new spending has a named offset, a sunset or a measured return.
- **Outcome 3:** A public service that is smaller in overhead, stronger in capability and clearer in accountability.
- **Outcome 4:** A budget the citizen can read: what they pay, what they get, and what changed.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Net debt-to-GSP stabilises below 25%; the operating balance is in surplus; the back-office share of spending falls; frontline output per dollar rises; and the Auditor-General no longer reports the same fragmentation findings year after year.

**What failure looks like:** Another decade of "efficiency dividends" that cut frontline capability, leave the same duplication in place, and shift costs to councils, households and future budgets.

**What the citizen experiences:** They see the services, not the machinery. When the machinery fails, they experience it as a wait, a form, a wrong number, a closed counter or a letter that contradicts the last one.
**What the frontline experiences:** Constant restructures, duplicated systems, consultant-led change, unfunded mandates, and a performance regime that measures activity rather than outcomes.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Entity sprawl:** About 306 listed departments, agencies and services, with overlapping mandates, boards, strategies, IT systems and corporate functions. [B]
- **Executive and corporate growth:** The 2026 reform program targets **332 executive and executive-like roles** (about **$359 million** over the forward estimates), a further **140 executive or executive-equivalent roles** (about **$100 million**), and changes to VPS5/VPS6 staffing (about **$125 million**). [A]
- **Consultant and contractor reliance:** The government is targeting a **$70 million** reduction in consultants and external advisers. A VAGO review found departments used **325 distinct consultant vendors** in 2023–24 and recommended more consistent reporting. [A]
- **Fragmented digital and shared services:** The 2026–27 Budget funds shared services and IT consolidation, with whole-of-government savings of **$37.6 million in 2026–27** rising to **$219.9 million in 2027–28**. The fact that this is still a reform in 2026 is the finding. [A]
- **Capital-operating gap:** Capital projects are approved with optimism, then their operating costs land on a later budget. Maintenance backlogs and cost blowouts are the visible symptom.
- **Tax expenditures:** Concessions, exemptions and loopholes are spending by another name, but they are rarely reviewed with the same rigour as direct grants.
- **Performance reporting:** Departments report outputs and activity; citizens need outcomes and experience. The gap makes it hard to stop what does not work.
- **Commonwealth cost-shifting:** National agreements and funding conditions can lock Victoria into delivery models that do not fit local needs.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** The government's own reform package claims more than **$4 billion** in savings. The independent question is how much is genuine productivity and how much is deferred capability. [B/C]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** The budget is a set of departmental envelopes; efficiency is a percentage cut applied to each; reform is a restructure.

**The new frame:** The budget is a **portfolio of outcomes**; the machinery is a **shared platform**; reform is a **portfolio reallocation** from low-value activity to high-value capability.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Efficiency dividend plus** | Across-the-board cuts, centralised approvals, more reporting | Fast fiscal control | Cuts frontline capability, rewards gaming, repeats every year | Not sufficient alone |
| **B. Outcome budget and shared platform** | Budgets allocated by outcome; common corporate/digital platforms; entities consolidated; sunset reviews | Durable savings, clearer accountability | Requires Cabinet discipline and a strong centre | Preferred base |
| **C. Public sector as a platform** | Small central core; empowered frontline teams; open contracts; citizen co-design; market and community delivery | High adaptability and trust | Hard to govern; risks fragmentation without strong standards | Use for selected services |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Stop budgeting for departments and start budgeting for outcomes.** A outcome budget does not abolish portfolios; it makes the cross-portfolio join the unit of accountability. The Treasurer becomes the steward of the whole system, not the sum of the parts.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Victoria Delivery Office:** a small, elite unit in DPC/DTF with the authority to challenge, stop, merge and sequence major projects and reforms. Not another coordinator — a decision unit with a published pipeline.
- **One back office, many front doors:** consolidate HR, finance, payroll, procurement, IT, legal and communications into shared services, with service-level agreements and public benchmarks.
- **Sunset by default:** every entity, board, program and tax concession has a five-year sunset unless re-approved against outcome evidence.
- **Capital rationing with whole-of-life budgets:** no project proceeds without a funded operating and maintenance plan.
- **A public sector capability account:** measure and report workforce skills, contract management, digital capability and delivery confidence alongside the fiscal accounts.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate overlapping public entities and boards (the government has announced 29 reductions) | DPC/DTF/portfolios | Redeploy, merge, or voluntary separation; protect frontline | Part of the $4b package; entity overhead and duplicated boards | [B] |
| Remove 332 executive/executive-like roles and a further 140 executive-equivalent roles | Departments | Targeted separations; no frontline impact | About $459m over forward estimates | [A] |
| Reduce consultant and external adviser spend by $70m | All departments | Bring work in-house; build permanent capability | $70m | [A] |
| Consolidate IT, networks, cloud, storage, security and desktop services | DGS | Redeploy digital staff; standardise platforms | $37.6m in 2026–27, $219.9m in 2027–28 | [A] |
| Merge duplicated HR, finance, legal, communications and policy units | Departments and agencies | Shared services; voluntary mobility | Estimated, to be costed | [D] |
| Sunset low-value grants, boards and programs | All portfolios | Reallocate staff; publish the list | To be identified in the first budget | [D] |
| Review tax expenditures with the same rigour as direct spending | DTF/Treasurer | No immediate workforce impact | Potentially significant; politically contested | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Executive reductions and entity mergers are contentious with the public sector unions and the affected boards. They are still right if the savings are real and the frontline is protected. The government must publish the baseline, the method and the redeployment numbers, not just the headline.
**What we will not cut:** Frontline service delivery; statutory integrity bodies; the capability needed to manage contracts, data and risk.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Outcome-based budget framework | Align money to results | DTF/Treasurer | Low | Low | Reallocation from low-value activity |
| Victoria Delivery Office | Stop, merge and sequence major reforms | DPC/DTF | Small | Small | Existing central agency capacity |
| Shared services utility | Cut duplication, standardise | DGS | Transition cost | Lower run cost | Duplicated corporate budgets |
| Common digital platforms (identity, payments, notifications, data) | Once-only, lower cost, better service | DGS | Capital | Lower run cost | Legacy IT and duplicated licences |
| Public sector capability academy | Contract management, digital, delivery, financial literacy | VPSC/DPC | Small | Small | Training and consultant spend |
| Public performance dashboard | Trust and accountability | DTF/DPC | Low | Low | Existing reporting |
| Sunset and review unit | Enforce the default | DTF/DPC | Small | Small | Entity and program savings |

**The new capability:** The public service must be able to **commission, contract, digitise, evaluate and stop**. Those are the capabilities that determine whether the money buys outcomes. They are also the capabilities most eroded by years of outsourcing.

**The new deal:** The citizen gets a budget they can read and services that improve. The public servant gets a clearer job, fewer restructures and better tools. The Treasurer gets a credible path to stabilisation.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Fair funding, fewer distorting conditions, tax reform cooperation | Better national outcomes, lower risk | Victoria absorbs cost-shifting and designs around conditions |
| Local government | Delivery partnership, local data, co-investment | Stable funding, less cost-shifting, shared services | Councils raise rates or cut services; trust falls |
| Unions / workforce | Support for redeployment and new skills | Job security, training, genuine consultation | Industrial action, implementation failure |
| Industry / consultants | Transparent pricing, capability transfer | A smaller but more strategic market | The state keeps buying dependence |
| Community sector | Delivery and lived experience | Multi-year outcomes funding, less reporting churn | Fragmentation and service gaps |
| Parliament / integrity bodies | Scrutiny and reform authority | Better information, stronger institutions | Reform is seen as spin |

**The convening mechanism:** A Cabinet sub-committee on fiscal strategy and delivery, chaired by the Treasurer, with the Premier and the Delivery Office.
**The accountability mechanism:** Quarterly public reporting against outcome budgets, entity consolidation, consultant spend, workforce capability and delivery confidence.

---

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Every portfolio** — the outcome budget is the operating system for all 16 portfolios. Without it, the cut/add ledger is just a list of good intentions.
- **Workforce and capability lever** — the savings depend on redeployment, retraining and new skills, not just separations.
- **Procurement and market design lever** — the state's purchasing power is the largest untapped policy instrument.
- **Digital, data and AI lever** — shared platforms and common data are the backbone of the back-office consolidation.
- **Integrity and trust lever** — efficiency without transparency becomes suspicion.

**The shared asset:** A single whole-of-government view of money, people, contracts, assets and outcomes. Build it once.

## 9. First 100 days

| Day | Move | Owner | Deliverable | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–30 | Publish the baseline: entity map, consultant spend, executive headcount, duplicated systems, tax expenditures | DTF/DGS/DPC | Public dashboard and Cabinet paper | Bureaucratic resistance |
| 1–30 | Establish the Victoria Delivery Office | Premier/Treasurer | Mandate, staff, pipeline | Turf war with departments |
| 31–60 | Announce the first sunset list: entities, boards, programs and concessions | DTF/DPC | Public list and consultation | Stakeholder backlash |
| 31–60 | Consolidate the first shared services tranche (payroll, finance, procurement) | DGS | Service-level agreement and plan | Union and system risk |
| 61–100 | Hand down an outcome-budget framework and a whole-of-life capital rule | Treasurer | Budget circular and pilot portfolios | Cabinet discipline |
| 61–100 | Publish a consultant and contractor register with rates, scope and outcomes | DGS | Open register | Commercial confidentiality claims |

**What we will not do in the first 100 days:** announce a mega-restructure without a transition plan; cut frontline roles to hit a headline; or promise savings that are actually deferred costs.

## 10. Measures that matter

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fiscal | Net debt-to-GSP | 24.8% (2026–27) | Stabilise below 25%, then decline | DTF | Quarterly |
| Fiscal | Interest expense | $8.9b (2026–27) | Reduce as share of revenue | DTF | Annual |
| Productivity | Back-office share of operating spend | To be baselined | Down | DTF/DGS | Annual |
| Workforce | Executive and corporate headcount | To be baselined | Down; frontline share up | VPSC | Quarterly |
| Capability | Contract management and digital capability | To be assessed | Up | VPSC | Annual |
| Trust | Citizen confidence in government services | To be baselined | Up | Survey | Annual |
| Integrity | Audit findings repeated year on year | To be baselined | Down | VAGO | Annual |
| Delivery | Major projects within approved budget and schedule | To be baselined | Up | Delivery Office | Quarterly |

**The one number that matters most:** Net debt-to-GSP, because it determines whether the state can keep investing in the things that matter.
**The leading indicator:** The share of new spending that has a named offset and a measured outcome.
**The kill switch:** If savings are being achieved by cutting frontline capability or deferring maintenance, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true split between genuine productivity savings and deferred costs in the 2026 reform package.
- The full whole-of-government consultant, contractor and labour-hire spend, by category and outcome.
- The number and cost of public entities beyond the 306 listed, including hospitals, schools, water corporations and cemetery trusts.
- The productivity effect of shared services in comparable jurisdictions.
- The distributional impact of tax expenditure reform.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A whole-of-government baseline of corporate functions, systems and contracts.
- A public sector capability census.
- A pilot outcome budget in two portfolios.
- An independent review of the reform savings methodology.
