# 12 — Regional Victoria and Agriculture

> **Citizen promise:** Strong regions, not just projects.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria funds regional development through dozens of grants, agencies and strategies, while the big levers — water, housing, transport, skills, energy and local government — are managed in Melbourne. The next move is **one place-based deal per region**, with a single budget, a single plan and a single accountable minister, backed by regional capitals as growth anchors.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Nine Victorian regions can receive up to **$1.55 million each** under Phase 2 of the Regional Drought Resilience Planning program. The **$50 million regional stream** of the Victorian Investment Fund supports business expansion, supply chains and agribusiness. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Rural and Regional Development, Agriculture and Water (Michaela Settle).
**Department(s):** Department of Jobs, Skills, Industry and Regions (DJSIR); Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA).
**Key agencies:** Regional Development Victoria (RDV); Agriculture Victoria; water corporations; catchment management authorities; regional partnerships; local councils; TAFEs; universities; First Peoples.
**Statute / framework:** Regional Development Act; Agriculture Victoria Act; Water Act; Catchment and Land Protection Act; planning schemes.
**Scale:** Nine regions; a large agricultural economy; significant water, energy and transport infrastructure. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Regions have growing, diversified economies and good jobs.
- **Outcome 2:** Agriculture is productive, resilient and sustainable.
- **Outcome 3:** Water is secure for households, agriculture, industry and the environment.
- **Outcome 4:** Regional towns have housing, services and transport.
- **Outcome 5:** Regions are prepared for drought, flood, fire and climate change.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Regional population and jobs grow; agricultural productivity and exports rise; water security improves; housing supply matches workforce needs; and regional liveability measures improve.

**What failure looks like:** More grant rounds, more plans and more pilot projects, but the same population decline, thin services and water uncertainty.

---

## 2. Current machinery

| Layer | Who / what | Role | Spend or scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regional development | DJSIR / RDV | Grants, investment, coordination | Regional |
| Agriculture | Agriculture Victoria | Productivity, biosecurity, drought | Statewide |
| Water | DEECA / water corporations / CMAs | Supply, irrigation, catchment | Regional |
| Local | 48 regional and rural councils | Local infrastructure and services | Local |
| Regional bodies | Regional partnerships, RDV | Advice and coordination | Regional |
| Commonwealth | Drought, water, infrastructure, agriculture | National programs | National |
| First Peoples | Traditional owners / RAPs | Cultural land and water | Place-based |

**What the citizen experiences:** A maze of grant programs, unclear eligibility and short-term funding. A farmer deals with water, drought, biosecurity and workforce separately. A family in a regional town may struggle to find housing, childcare or a GP.
**What the frontline experiences:** Thin markets, workforce shortages, long travel distances, fragmented programs and constant reporting.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Fragmented grants:** Many small programs with separate applications, reporting and timeframes.
- **Thin markets:** Regional providers cannot sustain services without aggregation or guaranteed demand.
- **Workforce and housing:** Regions cannot attract workers without housing, childcare, health and transport.
- **Water uncertainty:** Irrigation, environmental flows, town supply and drought resilience are managed separately.
- **Agricultural productivity:** Research, extension and adoption are fragmented.
- **Transport and freight:** Poor connectivity raises costs and limits access.
- **Digital gaps:** Connectivity and digital services lag metropolitan areas.
- **Disaster recovery:** Repeated recovery programs rather than resilience investment.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are duplicated programs, thin-market premiums, population decline and disaster recovery. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Regional development is a set of grants administered from Melbourne.

**The new frame:** Each region has a **single place-based deal**: one plan, one budget, one accountability framework, negotiated with the region and delivered by a regional board.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More grants** | Expand existing programs | Familiar | Fragmentation and short-termism | Not sufficient |
| **B. Regional deals** | Pooled budget, one plan, one board | Integrated, accountable | Requires devolution and trust | Preferred base |
| **C. Regional capitals** | Concentrate investment in major regional cities as anchors | Scale and services | Smaller towns may feel left out | Strong complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **One deal per region.** Pool the regional development, water, housing, skills, transport and energy budgets into a single negotiated package with outcomes, not a menu of grants.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Regional capitals:** Invest in Bendigo, Ballarat, Geelong, Latrobe, Shepparton, Mildura, Wodonga, Warrnambool and others as service and employment anchors.
- **Agriculture as advanced manufacturing:** value-add, food processing, agtech, biosecurity and export.
- **Water portfolio:** secure entitlements, efficiency, storage and environmental flows.
- **Regional housing and workforce:** housing, childcare, health and skills together.
- **Digital and freight:** connectivity and logistics.
- **Drought and disaster resilience:** invest before the event.

---

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate regional grant programs into place deals | RDV/DJSIR | Fewer applications, one plan | Admin cost | [D] |
| Merge small regional bodies and advisory committees | DJSIR/RDV | Shared services; fewer boards | Governance cost | [D] |
| Sunset low-value agricultural and regional grants | DJSIR/Agriculture Victoria | Reallocate to priorities | Program savings | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate water and catchment planning | DEECA/CMAs | One plan per catchment | Admin cost | [D] |
| Stop funding projects without a workforce and housing plan | RDV | Better sequencing | Avoided operating cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Pooling budgets and devolving decisions will be resisted by departments and some regional bodies. It requires clear accountability and a strong regional board.
**What we will not cut:** Drought support; water security; biosecurity; regional health and education access.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Regional place deals | One plan, one budget | RDV/regional boards | Moderate | Operating | Program consolidation |
| Regional capitals investment | Anchors for jobs and services | RDV/DJSIR | Capital | Operating | Existing programs |
| Agriculture value-add strategy | Productivity and exports | Agriculture Victoria/DJSIR | Moderate | Operating | Industry support |
| Water security and efficiency | Supply and resilience | DEECA/water corporations | Capital | Operating | New supply costs |
| Regional housing and workforce package | Attract and retain workers | DFFH/RDV/DJSIR | Capital | Operating | Vacancy and turnover |
| Digital and freight connectivity | Access and productivity | DJSIR/DTP | Capital | Operating | Connectivity gaps |
| Drought and disaster resilience | Reduce recovery costs | DEECA/RDV | Moderate | Operating | Disaster recovery |

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **negotiate place deals, pool budgets, devolve decisions and measure regional outcomes**.

**The new deal:** The region gets a single plan and budget. The state gets better outcomes per dollar. The community gets certainty.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Drought, water, infrastructure funding | Better regional outcomes | State fills gaps |
| Local government | Local plans and delivery | Stable funding, shared services | Fragmentation |
| Industry / farmers | Investment, productivity, stewardship | Water, skills, infrastructure | Decline |
| Water corporations / CMAs | Supply and catchment management | Funding and coordination | Conflict |
| TAFEs / universities | Skills and research | Industry partnerships | Workforce gaps |
| First Peoples | Cultural land and water authority | Self-determination | Conflict and loss |
| Regional boards | Local leadership | Decision-making power | Melbourne-centric decisions |
| Communities | Engagement | Better services | Trust falls |

**The convening mechanism:** A Regional Deals Cabinet Committee and regional boards with pooled budgets and outcome agreements.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public regional dashboard of jobs, population, housing, water, skills and liveability.

---

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Housing and planning** — regional housing and workforce.
- **Transport and roads** — connectivity and freight.
- **Skills, jobs and industry** — regional workforce and industry.
- **Energy and climate** — renewable projects and transition.
- **Environment, water and circular economy** — water and catchment.
- **Emergency management** — drought, flood and fire.
- **Local government** — delivery partners.

**The shared asset:** A single regional plan, budget and dashboard per region.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The total value and duplication of regional grants.
- The regional housing and workforce gap.
- The water yield and security by region.
- The productivity potential of agriculture.
- The best model for regional devolution.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A regional grants audit.
- A regional housing and workforce study.
- A water security review.
- Three place deal pilots.
