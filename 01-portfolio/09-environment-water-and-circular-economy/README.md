# 09 — Environment, Water and Circular Economy

> **Citizen promise:** Clean air, water and materials, with less waste.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria treats waste, water, catchments and climate as separate programs run by separate agencies. The next move is a **materials and natural-capital system**: price the waste, make producers responsible, count the value of nature, and manage water as one shared portfolio across households, agriculture, industry and the environment.
>
> **2026 baseline:** More than **3.33 billion containers** had been returned through CDS Vic by 30 June 2026. The state's circular-economy plan targets **80% diversion from landfill by 2030**, supported by the four-bin household system and producer responsibility. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Environment, Climate Action and Energy (Jaclyn Symes); Minister for Water, Agriculture and Regional Development (Michaela Settle).
**Department(s):** Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA).
**Key agencies:** Environment Protection Authority Victoria (EPA); Parks Victoria; water corporations; catchment management authorities (CMAs); Sustainability Victoria; Recycling Victoria; CDS Vic; VicWaste?; local councils.
**Statute / framework:** Environment Protection Act; Circular Economy (Waste Reduction and Recycling) Act; Water Act; Catchment and Land Protection Act; Murray–Darling Basin Plan; Climate Change Act.
**Scale:** Statewide waste, water, catchment, parks and environment portfolio; significant local government delivery role. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Less waste, more reuse and recycling, and a functioning circular materials market.
- **Outcome 2:** Safe, reliable and affordable water for households, agriculture, industry and the environment.
- **Outcome 3:** Healthy rivers, catchments, parks and biodiversity.
- **Outcome 4:** Clean air and reduced pollution.
- **Outcome 5:** Natural capital is measured, valued and maintained.

**What success looks like by 2030:** 80% diversion from landfill; lower contamination; more recycled content in public procurement; healthier waterways; secure water entitlements; and a natural-capital account that informs decisions.

**What failure looks like:** More bins, more landfill, more water restrictions, degraded rivers and another generation of environment programs without measurable outcomes.

**What the citizen experiences:** Confusing bin rules, rising waste charges, water restrictions, polluted waterways, and parks that are sometimes well maintained and sometimes not.
**What the frontline experiences:** Fragmented responsibilities, thin markets for recycled materials, aging infrastructure, and regulatory complexity.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Waste infrastructure gaps:** Recycling and organics processing capacity has not kept pace with collection.
- **Contamination:** Poor separation raises processing costs and reduces material value.
- **Producer responsibility:** Producers do not bear the full cost of packaging and products at end of life.
- **Water inefficiency:** Leakage, aging assets and inefficient irrigation.
- **Catchment fragmentation:** Water, land, biodiversity and planning are managed by different bodies.
- **Natural capital invisible:** Nature's value is not in the budget or the balance sheet, so it is degraded by default.
- **Parks maintenance:** Deferred maintenance and visitor pressure.
- **Climate adaptation:** Flood, fire and heat risks are not consistently integrated into land use and infrastructure.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are landfill, contamination, water losses, degraded catchments and disaster recovery. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Waste is a collection service; water is a utility; environment is a regulator and a set of parks.

**The new frame:** Waste is a **materials system**; water is a **shared portfolio**; nature is **infrastructure** with measurable value.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More collection and regulation** | More bins, more rules, more grants | Familiar | Does not fix markets or producers | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. Circular materials system** | Producer responsibility, recycled-content procurement, infrastructure, markets | Durable waste reduction | Requires national coordination and investment | Preferred base |
| **C. Natural capital and water accounts** | Value nature and water in budgets and decisions | Better long-term decisions | Methodological complexity | Essential complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Make the producer pay, and make the state buy recycled.** Extended producer responsibility for packaging and products, combined with recycled-content procurement, creates demand and funds the system.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Circular procurement:** government buys recycled materials, remanufactured goods and low-waste services.
- **Producer responsibility:** packaging, electronics, batteries, textiles and construction materials.
- **Water portfolio:** one plan across households, agriculture, industry, environment and Traditional Owners.
- **Natural capital accounts:** measure and report soil, water, biodiversity and carbon.
- **Catchment deals:** place-based agreements with CMAs, councils, farmers and First Peoples.
- **Deposit scheme expansion:** more materials, more return points, better data.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate overlapping waste and sustainability programs | DEECA/Recycling Victoria/Sustainability Victoria | Shared services; one front door | Admin cost | [D] |
| Remove subsidies that favour landfill or virgin materials | DEECA/EPA | Targeted transition support | Market distortion | [D] |
| Merge small water and catchment bodies where scale is weak | DEECA/water corporations | Redeploy; protect local jobs | Overhead and duplication | [D] |
| Sunset low-value environment grants | DEECA | Reallocate to catchment deals | Program savings | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate reporting across water, waste and environment | DEECA/EPA | Streamlined data | Admin time | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Producer responsibility and landfill pricing will be contested by industry. Water reform will be contested by irrigators and rural communities. Both need a fair transition.
**What we will not cut:** Safe drinking water; waste collection; environmental regulation; Traditional Owner rights.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extended producer responsibility scheme | Make producers pay | DEECA/EPA | Low | Operating | Waste system costs |
| Circular procurement policy | Create demand for recycled materials | DGS/DEECA | Low | Operating | Materials cost |
| Recycling and organics infrastructure | Close the capacity gap | Recycling Victoria/industry | Capital | Operating | Landfill and processing |
| Water efficiency and leakage program | Secure supply | Water corporations | Capital | Operating | New supply costs |
| Natural capital accounts | Value nature | DEECA/CMAs | Low | Operating | Better decisions |
| Catchment deals | Place-based delivery | CMAs/councils/First Peoples | Moderate | Operating | Program duplication |
| Parks maintenance guarantee | Visitor safety and conservation | Parks Victoria | Capital | Operating | Deferred maintenance |

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **run material markets, regulate producers, account for natural capital and coordinate water**.

**The new deal:** The household gets simpler bins and lower waste costs over time. The farmer gets water certainty. The community gets healthy rivers and parks. The state gets a circular economy and lower disaster costs.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Basin Plan, national waste policy, funding | Better environmental and water outcomes | State acts alone |
| Local government | Collection, local planning, enforcement | Infrastructure funding, shared standards | Contamination and cost |
| Industry / producers | Design for reuse, EPR fees | Market certainty, recycled content | Regulation and cost |
| Water corporations | Efficiency, investment, pricing | Stable regulation | Supply constraints |
| Farmers / irrigators | Water efficiency, land stewardship | Water security, support | Conflict and degradation |
| CMAs / parks | Catchment and land management | Funding and coordination | Fragmentation |
| First Peoples | Cultural land and water knowledge | Self-determination, joint management | Cultural loss and conflict |
| Households | Separation and conservation | Lower costs, healthier environment | Contamination and restrictions |

**The convening mechanism:** A Materials and Water Board with DEECA, EPA, councils, industry, water corporations, CMAs, farmers and First Peoples.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of diversion, contamination, water security, river health, emissions and parks condition.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Energy and climate** — emissions, adaptation and land use.
- **Housing and planning** — water, waste and green infrastructure in precincts.
- **Regional Victoria and agriculture** — water and catchment outcomes.
- **Local government** — collection and local environment.
- **Procurement and market design** — recycled content and circular markets.
- **Fiscal and public sector** — natural capital and whole-of-life infrastructure.

**The shared asset:** A single materials, water and natural-capital account.

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Waste | Diversion from landfill | To be baselined | 80% by 2030 | EPA/DEECA | Annual |
| Waste | Contamination rate | To be baselined | Down | councils/EPA | Quarterly |
| Materials | Recycled content in government procurement | To be baselined | Up | DGS | Annual |
| Water | Water security / storage | To be baselined | Safe | water corporations | Monthly |
| Water | Leakage | To be baselined | Down | water corporations | Annual |
| Environment | River and catchment health | To be baselined | Up | CMAs/EPA | Annual |
| Nature | Natural capital accounts | Not established | Published | DEECA | Annual |
| Parks | Visitor and maintenance condition | To be baselined | Up | Parks Victoria | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** Diversion from landfill, because it measures the whole materials system.
**The leading indicator:** Contamination rate at the kerb.
**The kill switch:** If diversion is achieved by exporting waste or burning recyclables, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true cost of the recycling and organics capacity gap.
- The best EPR model for each material.
- The water yield and efficiency potential.
- The method and reliability of natural capital accounts.
- The condition of parks and catchment assets.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A materials flow and capacity study.
- A water efficiency audit.
- A natural capital pilot.
- A catchment deal trial.
