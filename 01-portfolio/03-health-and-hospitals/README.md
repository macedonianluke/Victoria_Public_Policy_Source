# 03 — Health and Hospitals

> **Citizen promise:** Care when you need it, close to home.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria does not have a hospital problem. It has a **flow problem**. Ambulance, emergency, beds, elective surgery, community care and aged care are managed as separate queues by separate organisations. Run them as **one flow system**, buy capacity where it is cheapest, and move care closer to home.
>
> **2026 baseline:** **72%** of ambulance patients were transferred into emergency care within 40 minutes in the June quarter of 2026, against a **90% target**. In October 2026, **28%** of ambulance arrivals still exceeded the 40-minute benchmark. [A/B]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Health, Mental Health, Ambulance Services and Special Minister of State (Ingrid Stitt).
**Department(s):** Department of Health (DH).
**Key agencies:** Ambulance Victoria; public health services and hospitals; Safer Care Victoria; Victorian Agency for Health Information (VAHI); Primary Health Networks (Commonwealth); community health services; private hospitals.
**Statute / framework:** Health Services Act; Ambulance Services Act; Mental Health and Wellbeing Act; National Health Reform Agreement; Medicare.
**Scale:** One of the largest areas of state spending, with a workforce of more than 100,000 across public health services. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** People get timely emergency and elective care.
- **Outcome 2:** Care is safe, high quality and close to home.
- **Outcome 3:** The system flows: ambulance, ED, beds, discharge, community and aged care work as one.
- **Outcome 4:** Prevention and primary care reduce avoidable demand.
- **Outcome 5:** The workforce is stable, supported and sustainably rostered.

**What success looks like by 2029:** 90% of ambulance handovers within 40 minutes; elective surgery waiting lists within clinically recommended times; fewer avoidable admissions; more care delivered at home; and a measurable reduction in agency and locum reliance.

**What failure looks like:** More beds, more ambulances and more agency staff, but the same ramping, the same cancelled surgeries and the same exhausted workforce.

**What the citizen experiences:** Calling an ambulance and waiting; arriving at ED and waiting again; being admitted to a corridor; being discharged without a plan; waiting for elective surgery; repeating their story to every service.
**What the frontline experiences:** Bed block, manual bed finding, fragmented IT, agency shifts, unsafe rostering, administrative burden and a culture of blame.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Front-door congestion:** Ambulance offload delays and ED overcrowding are symptoms of bed and discharge blockage, not just demand.
- **Bed block:** Patients who no longer need acute care cannot leave because aged care, NDIS, housing or community supports are unavailable.
- **Fragmented health services:** Multiple health services duplicate corporate services, IT, procurement and back-office functions.
- **Agency and locum reliance:** Expensive staffing fills rosters but does not build a sustainable workforce.
- **Elective surgery queues:** Operating theatres and beds are not managed as a statewide throughput system.
- **Avoidable admissions:** Chronic disease, mental health and social needs present late because primary and community care are fragmented.
- **Capital duplication:** New buildings are approved without a plan for the workforce and operating costs to run them.
- **Data gaps:** Bed, workforce, demand and outcome data are not real-time or shared across the system.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The biggest avoidable costs are bed days, agency premiums, cancelled surgery, ambulance ramping and preventable admissions. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Each hospital, ambulance service and community provider manages its own queue and budget.

**The new frame:** The state manages **one patient flow system** across the whole continuum, with a single command view of demand, capacity and discharge.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More beds and ambulances** | Increase capacity in the current model | Visible; politically simple | Expensive; does not fix flow or workforce | Necessary in places, not sufficient |
| **B. One flow system** | Real-time bed and demand management; discharge partnerships; hospital at home; common rostering | Improves flow without building everything | Requires data, trust and cross-agency authority | Preferred base |
| **C. Integrated care system** | Capitated budget across primary, acute, community and aged care | Strong prevention and continuity | Major Commonwealth/state complexity | Long-term direction |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Buy capacity, not buildings.** Use public, private, community and home-based capacity where it delivers the best outcome. The state's job is to commission the system, not to own every asset.

**Supporting moves:**

- **One flow command centre:** real-time visibility of beds, ED, ambulance, elective and discharge across the state.
- **Hospital at home:** scale acute and sub-acute care in the home with remote monitoring and clinical support.
- **Common rostering and bed management platform:** one system, not 20.
- **Elective surgery throughput:** dedicated elective centres and statewide scheduling.
- **Front-door urgent care:** GP-led urgent care, community clinics and alternatives to ED.
- **Discharge-to-home packages:** housing, aged care, NDIS and community support arranged before discharge.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate corporate and IT functions across health services | Health services/DH | Shared services; redeploy | Lower overhead, common systems | [D] |
| Reduce agency and locum reliance with permanent recruitment and rostering reform | Health services | Better shifts, stable teams | Agency premium | [D] |
| Stop low-volume, high-cost services where safe and accessible alternatives exist | Health services | Redeploy clinicians; regional access guarantees | Cost and quality | [D] |
| Reduce avoidable admissions through community and primary care | DH/PHNs/community | More care at home | Bed days | [D] |
| Standardise procurement of devices, consumables and pharmaceuticals | DH/HealthShare Victoria | Supplier changes | Bulk purchasing | [D] |
| Sunset duplicated quality and data reporting | DH/SCV/VAHI | Streamlined reporting | Admin time | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Consolidating services and reducing agency reliance will be contested by unions, clinicians and local communities. The state must guarantee access, consult genuinely and invest in the transition.
**What we will not cut:** Emergency care; clinical safety; rural access; mental health crisis care.

**The new capability:** The system must be able to **see demand, move capacity, manage discharge and commission care** across organisational boundaries.

**The new deal:** The citizen gets care closer to home and shorter waits. The clinician gets a safer, better-resourced system. The hospital gets flow. The Treasurer gets a lower cost per episode.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Medicare, aged care, NDIS, primary care reform | Better patient flow and outcomes | States absorb avoidable acute costs |
| Health services | Data, bed visibility, discharge discipline | Shared capacity and funding | Ramping persists |
| Ambulance Victoria | Demand data, alternative pathways | Faster handover, fewer lost hours | Ambulance fleet tied up at ED |
| Private hospitals | Capacity and elective throughput | Purchasing certainty | Public waitlists grow |
| Primary care / PHNs | Urgent care, chronic disease management | Funding and integration | Avoidable admissions rise |
| Unions / workforce | Rostering reform and training | Safer workloads, permanent jobs | Agency reliance and burnout |
| Community / aged care / NDIS | Discharge support | Funding and coordination | Bed block continues |
| Patients / families | Feedback and co-design | Better experience | Trust falls |

**The convening mechanism:** A Health System Flow Board, chaired by DH, with ambulance, health services, primary care, aged care, NDIS and community providers.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of ambulance handover, ED wait, bed occupancy, elective wait, discharge delays and agency spend.

---

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Mental health and wellbeing** — a large share of ED and crisis demand is mental health; integrate the front door.
- **Families, children and community** — aged care, disability and child protection transitions determine bed block.
- **Housing and planning** — patients cannot leave hospital without a home.
- **Workforce and capability** — the health workforce pipeline is the binding constraint.
- **Digital, data and AI** — real-time flow requires common data and identity.
- **Transport and roads** — ambulance response and patient transport depend on the road network.
- **Fiscal and public sector** — health is the largest budget; productivity and outcomes must be measured together.

**The shared asset:** One real-time view of demand, beds, workforce and discharge across the state.

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Flow | Bed occupancy | To be baselined | Safe range | DH | Daily |
| Quality | Avoidable readmissions | To be baselined | Down | VAHI | Quarterly |
| Workforce | Agency/locum share of hours | To be baselined | Down | DH | Quarterly |
| Cost | Cost per episode / per outcome | To be baselined | Down | DH | Annual |
| Experience | Patient-reported experience | To be baselined | Up | VAHI | Quarterly |
| Equity | Rural and outer-metro access | To be baselined | Up | DH | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** Ambulance handover within 40 minutes, because it reveals the whole system's flow.
**The leading indicator:** The number of patients waiting in hospital for a non-acute placement.
**The kill switch:** If flow improves by rationing care or shifting costs to patients, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true cost of bed block by cause (aged care, NDIS, housing, community support).
- The productivity effect of common rostering and bed management.
- The safe and feasible scale of hospital at home.
- The optimal mix of public, private and community capacity.
- The workforce pipeline by discipline and region.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A whole-of-system flow audit.
- A hospital-at-home evaluation.
- A workforce census and pipeline model.
- A costing of avoidable admissions and discharges.
