# 05 — Education and Early Childhood

> **Citizen promise:** Every child ready for school and life.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria runs early childhood, schools, TAFE and universities as separate systems with separate funding, data and accountability. The next move is **one learning system from 0 to 18**, with a teacher pipeline treated as infrastructure, schools as community hubs, and funding that follows need — not postcode or history.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria's workforce forecast projects **50,234 secondary teachers available against demand for 52,895** in 2026 — a shortfall of about **2,661**. Public schools are on a path to **75% of the Schooling Resource Standard**, with full funding not scheduled until **2034** under the 2026 Commonwealth agreement. [A/B]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Education, Skills and Training, First Peoples and Women and Gender Equality (Gabrielle Williams).
**Department(s):** Department of Education (DE).
**Key agencies:** Victorian Curriculum and Assessment Authority (VCAA); Victorian School Building Authority (VSBA); Victorian Registration and Qualifications Authority (VRQA); schools; early childhood services; TAFEs; universities; Catholic and independent school systems.
**Statute / framework:** Education and Training Reform Act; National School Reform Agreement; Early Childhood Education and Care National Agreement; Child Safe Standards.
**Scale:** About 1,500 government schools; a large early childhood system; TAFE network; significant capital and workforce footprint. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Children arrive at school ready to learn.
- **Outcome 2:** Every student has a great teacher and a safe, well-maintained school.
- **Outcome 3:** Learning is personalised, inclusive and connected to life and work.
- **Outcome 4:** The system closes the gap for disadvantaged, regional, First Peoples and disabled students.
- **Outcome 5:** Schools are community hubs that connect families to health, wellbeing and support.

**What success looks like by 2032:** All public schools funded to the SRS; teacher vacancies at sustainable levels; improved NAPLAN and completion; narrower equity gaps; every school well maintained; and more students leaving with a pathway to work, TAFE or university.

**What failure looks like:** More funding announcements, the same teacher shortages, crumbling buildings and widening gaps.

**What the citizen experiences:** A child may attend early childhood, kindergarten, school, outside-school care and tutoring through different systems with different forms and waiting lists. A family in a growth area may wait for a school. A teacher may be on a short-term contract for years.
**What the frontline experiences:** Teacher shortages, administrative load, poor buildings, large classes, inequitable funding and constant policy change.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Teacher pipeline:** Secondary teacher shortages, especially in maths, science, languages, special education and regional schools. [A]
- **Funding delay:** The gap to full SRS funding is spread to 2034, leaving schools to manage with less. [A]
- **School maintenance:** Deferred maintenance creates unsafe and unsuitable learning environments and higher long-term costs.
- **Fragmented early childhood:** Kindergarten, childcare, maternal and child health, and early intervention are separate systems with different funding and data.
- **Administrative burden:** Teachers spend too much time on compliance, data entry and reporting.
- **Inequity:** Disadvantage, disability, regionality and First Peoples status still predict outcomes.
- **Capital planning:** New schools are built without the workforce, transport and community infrastructure to make them work.
- **Post-school pathways:** VET, TAFE and university are poorly connected to school and industry.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The biggest costs are teacher turnover, poor school readiness, disengagement, duplication and deferred maintenance. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Early childhood, schools and post-school education are separate systems, each funded and managed separately.

**The new frame:** A **0–18 learning system** with a shared data platform, a shared workforce strategy, a shared capital plan and a shared equity target.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More funding, same structure** | Increase SRS, build schools, recruit teachers | Addresses inputs | Does not fix fragmentation or inequity | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. One learning system 0–18** | Shared data, workforce, capital, early childhood integration | Better transitions, less duplication | Requires cross-agency governance | Preferred base |
| **C. Community learning hubs** | Schools open to families and community services; integrated early years | Strong prevention and trust | Complex operations | Strong local model |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Treat the teacher pipeline as infrastructure.** Plan, fund and deliver teachers the way the state plans roads and hospitals: forecast demand, fund places, provide housing and relocation, and track supply by subject and region.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Early childhood integration:** one enrolment, one data system, one workforce strategy across kindergarten, childcare and early intervention.
- **School as community hub:** co-locate maternal and child health, allied health, family services, mental health and adult learning.
- **Needs-based funding:** weight funding to disadvantage, disability, remoteness and English as an additional language.
- **Common learning platform:** curriculum, assessment, student records and family communication in one place.
- **School maintenance guarantee:** a funded, published maintenance standard.
- **Pathways guarantee:** every student leaves school with a plan for work, TAFE or university.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicated early childhood data and enrolment systems | DE/DFFH | Shared platform; redeploy | Admin time and cost | [D] |
| Reduce low-value compliance and reporting | DE/VCAA/VRQA | Streamlined reporting | Teacher time | [D] |
| Merge or share small school back-office functions | DE/schools | Shared services | Admin cost | [D] |
| Stop building schools without a workforce and transport plan | DE/VSBA/DTP | Better sequencing | Avoided operating cost | [D] |
| Sunset programs without evidence of learning gain | DE | Reallocate to proven interventions | Program savings | [D] |

**The hard calls:** School closures and mergers are deeply contested. They should be a last resort, co-designed with communities, and only where access and quality can be guaranteed.
**What we will not cut:** Classroom teaching; student wellbeing; support for disadvantaged students; early childhood access.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher pipeline plan | Supply by subject and region | DE/universities/TAFEs | Moderate | Operating | Reduced turnover and agency |
| Early childhood integration | One system for families | DE/DFFH | Moderate | Operating | Duplicated administration |
| School as community hub program | Co-located services | DE/health/community | Capital and operating | Operating | Better outcomes, less duplication |
| Common learning platform | One record, less admin | DE | Capital | Lower run cost | Legacy systems |
| Maintenance guarantee | Safe, suitable schools | VSBA/DE | Capital | Operating | Deferred maintenance |
| Needs-based funding | Equity | DE/Commonwealth | Recurrent | Recurrent | Reallocation |
| Pathways guarantee | Work, TAFE, university | DE/DJSIR/TAFEs | Low | Operating | Better completion |

**The new capability:** The system must be able to **forecast workforce, integrate early years, manage assets and use data to target need**.

**The new deal:** The child gets continuity and support. The family gets one system. The teacher gets a manageable workload and a career path. The state gets better outcomes per dollar.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Full SRS funding, early childhood agreement, university places | Better outcomes and productivity | States fill the gap or accept inequity |
| Catholic / independent systems | Collaboration on data, workforce, access | Fair funding and planning | Segmented system |
| Universities | Teacher education places and quality | Workforce planning certainty | Shortages persist |
| TAFEs | Pathways and applied learning | Enrolment and industry links | Disengagement |
| Unions / teachers | Reform engagement | Workload reduction, pay and conditions | Industrial action |
| Councils | Early childhood infrastructure and community hubs | Funding and shared facilities | Fragmentation |
| Families / students | Engagement | Better experience | Trust falls |
| Employers | Pathways, work experience, apprenticeships | Skilled workforce | Skills gaps |

**The convening mechanism:** A Victorian Learning System Board with DE, early childhood, schools, TAFEs, universities, industry, unions, councils and families.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public learning dashboard from early childhood to post-school, with equity breakdowns.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Skills, jobs and industry** — TAFE, apprenticeships and pathways.
- **Health and mental health** — student wellbeing and school health.
- **Families, children and community** — early intervention and child protection.
- **Housing and planning** — schools in growth areas.
- **Regional Victoria** — workforce and access.
- **Workforce and capability** — teacher pipeline.
- **Digital, data and AI** — common learning platform.

**The shared asset:** A single learner record and a single workforce and capital plan from 0 to 18.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true cost of the maintenance backlog.
- The supply and retention of teachers by subject and region.
- The effect of early childhood integration on outcomes.
- The best model for school-community hubs.
- The demand for post-school pathways.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A teacher workforce census.
- A school asset condition audit.
- An early childhood integration pilot.
- A pathways evaluation.
