# 13 — Emergency Management and Resilience

> **Citizen promise:** Prepared, warned, supported, rebuilt.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria manages fire, flood, storm and heat through separate agencies, plans and funding streams, then spends heavily on recovery. The next move is **all hazards, one system**: risk-based investment, community resilience, volunteer sustainability and land-use decisions that stop creating future disaster costs.
>
> **2026 baseline:** More than **200 bush and grass fires** ignited across Victoria on **9 January 2026**, and about **440,000 hectares** had burned by February. The 2026–27 Budget committed **$217 million** for emergency-services capability and a further **$422 million** for response and recovery. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Police, Emergency Services, Multicultural Affairs and Crime Prevention (Paul Edbrooke).
**Department(s):** Department of Justice and Community Safety (DJCS).
**Key agencies:** Emergency Management Victoria (EMV); Country Fire Authority (CFA); Fire Rescue Victoria (FRV); Victoria State Emergency Service (VICSES); Forest Fire Management Victoria (FFMVic); Life Saving Victoria; Parks Victoria; local councils; Bureau of Meteorology (Commonwealth); Australian Defence Force.
**Statute / framework:** Emergency Management Act; Country Fire Authority Act; Fire Rescue Victoria Act; State Emergency Service Act; Planning and Environment Act; Climate Change Act.
**Scale:** Statewide emergency management system with career and volunteer agencies, heavy fleet and significant recovery spending. [A]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Communities are prepared and warned.
- **Outcome 2:** Response is fast, coordinated and safe.
- **Outcome 3:** Recovery is timely and people-centred.
- **Outcome 4:** Risk is reduced before the event, not just managed after.
- **Outcome 5:** Volunteers are supported and sustainable.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Fewer preventable losses; faster warnings; better community preparedness; sustainable volunteer numbers; and lower recovery costs through resilience investment.

**What failure looks like:** More response and recovery spending, the same communities rebuilt in the same risky places, and volunteers burned out.

| Coordination | EMV / DJCS | All-hazard coordination | Statewide |
| Fire | CFA / FRV / FFMVic | Fire response and land management | Statewide |
| Flood / storm | VICSES / councils | Response and recovery | Statewide |
| Water rescue | Life Saving Victoria | Coastal and water rescue | Statewide |

| Local | 79 councils | Local emergency management | Local |
| Commonwealth | BoM, ADF, disaster funding | National support | National |

**What the citizen experiences:** Warnings that may not reach them, evacuation confusion, insurance delays, and a recovery process that is slow and complex.
**What the frontline experiences:** Fragmented command, aging assets, volunteer shortages, short funding cycles and unclear accountability.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Fragmented governance:** Multiple agencies, plans and committees create coordination costs and unclear accountability.
- **Response-heavy spending:** Money is concentrated in response and recovery, not prevention and resilience.
- **Land-use risk:** New development in flood, fire and heat zones creates future costs.
- **Volunteer sustainability:** Recruitment, retention, training and fatigue.
- **Aging assets:** Fleet, stations, communications and equipment.
- **Insurance and recovery:** Complex claims, underinsurance and repeated rebuilds.
- **Warnings and data:** Inconsistent messaging and data sharing.
- **Climate change:** More frequent and severe events increase risk.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are recovery, insurance, asset replacement and lost productivity. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Emergency management is a set of response agencies coordinated after an event.

**The new frame:** Emergency management is a **risk and resilience system** that starts with land use, infrastructure, insurance and community preparedness.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More response capacity** | More trucks, stations and crews | Visible | Expensive; does not reduce risk | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. All-hazards resilience system** | Risk-based investment, prevention, community resilience, volunteer support | Lower long-term cost | Requires cross-agency discipline | Preferred base |
| **C. Community resilience hubs** | Local hubs with supplies, communications, energy and support | Local self-reliance | Coverage and standards | Strong complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Invest before the event.** A dollar spent on resilience, land-use planning and community preparedness can avoid several dollars of response and recovery.

**Supporting moves:**

- **All-hazards risk assessment:** one statewide risk picture, updated for climate.
- **Resilience investment framework:** fund prevention and risk reduction, not just response.
- **Land-use and insurance signals:** stop approving development in high-risk areas without mitigation.
- **Volunteer sustainability:** paid support, training, equipment and employer recognition.
- **Community resilience hubs:** local places with power, water, communications and support.
- **Recovery guarantee:** a single case manager and a clear timeline.
- **Real-time data and warnings:** one source of truth.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate emergency governance committees and plans | EMV/DJCS | Clearer accountability | Admin time | [D] |
| Stop approving development in high-risk areas without mitigation | DTP/councils | Safer communities | Avoided recovery cost | [D] |
| Sunset low-value recovery programs and duplicate grants | EMV/DJCS | Simpler, faster recovery | Admin cost | [D] |
| Reduce duplicated fleet and asset procurement | EMV/agencies | Shared fleet and standards | Procurement savings | [D] |
| Consolidate warnings and public information systems | EMV | One source of truth | Duplicated systems | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Land-use restrictions and insurance reform will be contested by developers, councils and homeowners. They are necessary to stop creating future risk.
**What we will not cut:** Response capacity; warnings; volunteer support; recovery assistance.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All-hazards risk and resilience plan | One risk picture | EMV/DJCS | Low | Operating | Duplicated planning |
| Resilience investment fund | Prevention | EMV/DEECA | Capital | Operating | Recovery costs |
| Community resilience hubs | Local preparedness | Councils/community | Capital | Operating | Response and recovery |
| Volunteer sustainability package | Recruitment and retention | CFA/FRV/VICSES | Moderate | Operating | Volunteer attrition |
| Fleet and asset renewal | Safe, reliable response | Agencies | Capital | Operating | Breakdown and replacement |
| Recovery guarantee | One case manager, clear timeline | EMV/DJCS | Low | Operating | Complaint and delay |
| Real-time warnings and data | One source of truth | EMV/BoM | Capital | Operating | Duplicated systems |

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **assess risk, invest in resilience, coordinate all hazards and support volunteers**.

**The new deal:** The community gets faster warnings, safer places and faster recovery. The volunteer gets support. The state gets lower long-term costs.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | BoM, ADF, disaster funding | Better national resilience | State absorbs costs |
| Local government | Local planning, response and recovery | Funding and support | Fragmentation |
| Agencies | Response, prevention, community engagement | Clear mandate and resources | Coordination failure |
| Volunteers | Time and skill | Training, equipment, support | Attrition |
| Insurers | Risk signals and claims | Better land-use and mitigation | Underinsurance and delay |
| Utilities | Resilience and restoration | Regulatory certainty | Outages |
| Communities | Preparedness and engagement | Safety and support | Loss |
| First Peoples | Cultural knowledge and land management | Self-determination | Cultural loss |

**The convening mechanism:** An Emergency Management and Resilience Board with EMV, agencies, councils, utilities, insurers, communities and First Peoples.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of preparedness, response, recovery, volunteer numbers and asset condition.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Energy and climate** — heat, fire, flood and grid resilience.
- **Housing and planning** — land-use risk and resilient buildings.
- **Local government** — local response and recovery.
- **Health and mental health** — disaster health and psychosocial support.
- **Families, children and community** — recovery support.
- **Transport and roads** — evacuation and access.
- **Regional Victoria** — drought, flood and fire.

**The shared asset:** A single all-hazards risk, asset and warning picture.

## 10. Measures that matter

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preparedness | Community preparedness | To be baselined | Up | EMV | Annual |
| Response | Time to warning and response | To be baselined | Down | EMV | Event-based |
| Recovery | Time to recovery support | To be baselined | Down | EMV | Event-based |
| Volunteers | Active volunteers | To be baselined | Up | agencies | Annual |
| Assets | Asset condition | To be baselined | Up | agencies | Annual |
| Risk | Development in high-risk areas | To be baselined | Down | DTP | Annual |
| Cost | Recovery cost per event | To be baselined | Down | EMV | Annual |
| Resilience | Mitigation investment | To be baselined | Up | EMV | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** Preventable losses per event, because it measures whether resilience is working.
**The leading indicator:** The share of new development in high-risk areas with mitigation.
**The kill switch:** If resilience investment is diverted to response, or volunteer numbers fall, stop and redesign.

---

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The full cost of response and recovery by hazard.
- The condition of the emergency asset fleet.
- The volunteer recruitment and retention drivers.
- The effect of land-use planning on risk.
- The best model for community resilience hubs.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- An all-hazards risk assessment.
- An asset condition audit.
- A volunteer workforce strategy.
- A resilience investment evaluation.
