# 06 — Skills, Jobs and Industry

> **Citizen promise:** A good job, and the skills to get it.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria funds training, industry support and employment services through separate programs, separate agencies and separate grant rounds. The next move is **employer-led place compacts**: one plan per region and priority industry, with TAFE as the anchor, a guaranteed apprenticeship pathway, and procurement used deliberately to build local capability.
>
> **2026 baseline:** About **196,000 new workers** are expected in occupations typically requiring VET qualifications over the next three years. The **Free TAFE Guarantee became law in April 2026**, and the 2026–27 Budget funds up to **59,000 additional subsidised training places**, including about **15,000 Free TAFE places**. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Skills and Training (Gabrielle Williams); Minister for Jobs, Industry and Advanced Manufacturing and Defence Industry (Melissa Horne); Minister for Small and Family Business, Trade, Tourism and Investment and Veterans (Natalie Suleyman).
**Department(s):** Department of Jobs, Skills, Industry and Regions (DJSIR).
**Key agencies:** Victorian Skills Authority; TAFE network; Jobs Victoria; Regional Development Victoria; industry bodies; universities; registered training organisations (RTOs); Local Learning and Employment Networks.
**Statute / framework:** Education and Training Reform Act; Free TAFE Guarantee; Victorian Skills Plan 2026–27; Industry Policy; Local Jobs First Act.
**Scale:** TAFE network, subsidised training places, industry programs and employment services across the state. [A]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Employers can fill critical roles with locally trained people.
- **Outcome 2:** Victorians can get the skills for a good job, regardless of where they live or where they start.
- **Outcome 3:** Training investment follows real demand, not provider history.
- **Outcome 4:** Industry policy builds durable comparative advantage, not just grants.
- **Outcome 5:** Small and family businesses can grow, hire and invest.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Skill shortages in priority occupations fall; VET completion and job outcomes rise; regional participation improves; apprenticeship commencements and completions grow; and local content in major projects increases.

**What failure looks like:** More training places, more grants and more strategies, but the same shortages, the same thin markets and the same regional gaps.

## 2. Current machinery

| Layer | Who / what | Role | Spend or scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skills | DJSIR / Victorian Skills Authority | Planning, funding, regulation | Statewide |
| Training | TAFEs and RTOs | Delivery | Mixed |
| Jobs | Jobs Victoria | Employment services | Statewide |
| Industry | DJSIR / industry bodies | Industry policy, grants, advocacy | Sectoral |
| Regional | Regional Development Victoria | Regional investment | Regional |
| Commonwealth | Employment, migration, universities | National settings | National |
| Employers | Industry, small business | Demand, work placements | Market |

**What the citizen experiences:** A confusing market of courses, providers, subsidies and job services. A jobseeker may be directed to training that does not lead to a job. An employer may train staff who leave for a competitor.
**What the frontline experiences:** Short funding cycles, changing priorities, thin regional markets, poor data on demand, and high administrative burden.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Skills mismatch:** Training places are not consistently aligned to real vacancies, especially in regional and priority industries.
- **Completion and job outcomes:** Course commencements are measured more than completions and employment.
- **Thin markets:** Regional and specialist training providers cannot sustain viable cohorts without coordination.
- **Fragmented programs:** Grants, employment services and industry support are delivered through separate processes with separate reporting.
- **Employer engagement:** Employers are consulted but not contracted to provide placements, mentoring or jobs.
- **Migration and workforce planning:** Migration settings are national, but the state does not plan the settlement, housing and skills support that make migration work.
- **Procurement leakage:** Major projects import skills and materials that could build local capability.
- **Small business support:** Fragmented grants and advisory services with low take-up.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The biggest costs are unfilled vacancies, low completion, repeated training, and imported labour and materials. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Government funds training places and industry grants; employers hire whoever is available.

**The new frame:** Government convenes an **employer-led compact** for each priority industry and region: here is the workforce demand, here is the training pipeline, here are the placements and jobs, here is the housing and transport support, here is the local content commitment.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More subsidised places** | Expand Free TAFE and training subsidies | Access and affordability | Does not guarantee demand or completion | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. Employer-led place compacts** | Industry, TAFE, government, unions agree targets and contributions | Demand-led, local, accountable | Requires coordination and data | Preferred base |
| **C. Skills accounts** | Individuals control a training entitlement | Choice and portability | Risk of low-value choices;thin markets | Useful complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Make TAFE the anchor institution of regional and industry policy.** TAFE campuses are public assets with workshops, teachers, industry links and community reach. Use them as the base for apprenticeships, micro-credentials, adult learning, small business advice and industry innovation.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Apprenticeship guarantee:** every eligible school leaver and career changer can access a paid apprenticeship or traineeship in a priority occupation.
- **Career accounts:** a portable entitlement for adults to retrain, with career guidance.
- **Local content and skills plans:** major projects must show how they build Victorian capability.
- **Migration and settlement plan:** align visa settings with housing, skills and regional needs.
- **Small business one-stop shop:** advice, finance and procurement in one place.
- **Outcome contracts for training:** pay providers for completion and job outcomes, not just commencements.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicated industry grants and advisory programs | DJSIR | One compact per industry/region | Admin cost and confusion | [D] |
| Stop funding low-value courses with poor completion and job outcomes | DJSIR/VSA | Reallocate to priority skills | Training subsidy | [D] |
| Merge small, overlapping industry bodies and boards | DJSIR | Shared services; fewer boards | Governance cost | [D] |
| Reduce reporting duplication across training and employment programs | DJSIR | Streamlined data | Provider time | [D] |
| Sunset place-based grants that do not have a workforce outcome | RDV/DJSIR | Reallocate to compacts | Program savings | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Closing courses and consolidating industry bodies will upset providers and boards. It is justified only if the savings are reinvested in completion, placements and regional access.
**What we will not cut:** Free TAFE places; apprenticeship support; regional access; support for disadvantaged learners.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Employer-led place compacts | Align training to jobs | DJSIR/TAFEs/industry | Low | Operating | Program consolidation |
| Apprenticeship guarantee | Pipeline for priority trades | DJSIR/TAFEs/employers | Moderate | Operating | Skill shortage costs |
| Career accounts | Adult retraining | DJSIR/VSA | Moderate | Operating | Existing subsidies |
| TAFE centres of excellence | Digital/AI, care, renewable energy | TAFEs/industry | Capital | Operating | Industry and training spend |
| Local content and skills plans | Build Victorian capability | DJSIR/Industry Capability Network | Low | Operating | Procurement leverage |
| Migration and settlement plan | Fill critical roles | DJSIR/Commonwealth | Low | Operating | Vacancy costs |
| Small business one-stop shop | Simplify support | DJSIR/Service Victoria | Low | Operating | Fragmented grants |

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **forecast demand, broker employer commitments, commission outcomes and connect skills to housing, transport and migration**.

**The new deal:** The worker gets a pathway to a job. The employer gets a reliable pipeline. The region gets an anchor institution. The state gets productivity and participation.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employers | Placements, jobs, mentoring, wage subsidies | Skilled workforce, local content | Shortages persist |
| Unions | Training and apprenticeship support | Good jobs, safety, wages | Skills agreements stall |
| TAFEs | Delivery, industry links, regional reach | Funding and capital | Underused public assets |
| Universities | Research, degrees, teacher education | Industry partnerships | Fragmented pathways |
| Commonwealth | Migration, employment services, funding | Better outcomes | State fills gaps |
| Local government | Land, planning, local coordination | Jobs and population | Fragmentation |
| Regional bodies | Local intelligence and convening | Investment | Thin markets |
| First Peoples | Cultural authority and workforce | Self-determination | Exclusion |

**The convening mechanism:** Industry and regional skills compacts, chaired by DJSIR with employers, TAFEs, unions, councils and Commonwealth.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public skills dashboard: vacancies, commencements, completions, job outcomes, wages and regional participation.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Education and early childhood** — pathways, VET in schools, careers.
- **Regional Victoria and agriculture** — regional workforce and industry compacts.
- **Housing and planning** — workers need homes; growth needs skills.
- **Transport and roads** — access to training and jobs.
- **Energy and climate** — renewable energy and electrification workforce.
- **Procurement and market design** — local content and skills plans.
- **Workforce and capability** — public sector and care workforce.

**The shared asset:** A single workforce demand and supply model by region, industry and occupation.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The real demand by occupation, region and industry.
- The completion and job outcomes of every subsidised course.
- The capacity of employers to provide placements.
- The effect of local content rules on cost and capability.
- The settlement needs of migrant workers.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A workforce demand model.
- A training outcome audit.
- Three place compacts.
- An apprenticeship guarantee pilot.
