# 07 — Transport and Roads

> **Citizen promise:** Move reliably, affordably and safely.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria manages transport as a set of projects and modes, not as a **mobility service**. The next move is to treat the network as one system: maintain what we have, price and manage demand, integrate fares and information, sequence precincts with housing, and apply ruthless assurance to mega-projects before they consume the next decade's capital.
>
> **2026 baseline:** The **Metro Tunnel** is a completed project with an estimated **$13.5 billion** total investment, up from $10.9 billion. **Suburban Rail Loop East** has a public ceiling of **$30–34.5 billion**, but the Auditor-General warns it is more likely than not to exceed it; the government has announced about **$2 billion** in proposed savings. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Roads and Road Safety (Paul Hamer); Minister for Public Transport and Creative Industries (Vicki Ward).
**Department(s):** Department of Transport and Planning (DTP).
**Key agencies:** Transport Victoria; Public Transport Victoria (PTV); Victorian Infrastructure Delivery Authority (VIDA); Suburban Rail Loop Authority (SRLA); VicRoads; local councils; freight and port operators; public transport operators.
**Statute / framework:** Transport Integration Act; Road Management Act; Suburban Rail Loop Act; national heavy vehicle and rail agreements.
**Scale:** One of the largest capital pipelines in the state; a road network of more than 200,000 km including local roads; a multi-modal public transport network. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** People and goods move reliably and safely.
- **Outcome 2:** The existing network is maintained before it is expanded.
- **Outcome 3:** Public and active transport are attractive, integrated and affordable.
- **Outcome 4:** Major projects are delivered on budget, on time and with benefits realised.
- **Outcome 5:** Transport, housing and land use are planned together.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Road condition improves; pothole repair is fast and accountable; public transport patronage grows; project cost and schedule variance falls; and new housing is matched with transport.

**What failure looks like:** Another mega-project, another blowout, another maintenance backlog, and a citizen who still cannot get to work on time.

## 2. Current machinery

| Layer | Who / what | Role | Spend or scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roads | DTP / VicRoads / councils | Construction, maintenance, safety | Large |
| Public transport | PTV / operators | Trains, trams, buses | Large |
| Major projects | VIDA / SRLA | Delivery | Mega-project |
| Planning | DTP | Land use and transport integration | Statewide |
| Freight | Ports, freight operators, DTP | Ports, rail, road freight | Sectoral |
| Local | 79 councils | Local roads and paths | Local |
| Commonwealth | Infrastructure Australia / funding | National projects and reform | National |

**What the citizen experiences:** Congestion, unreliable services, crowded trains, poor bus frequency, expensive fares, potholes, and projects that take a decade. They do not experience "modes"; they experience a journey.
**What the frontline experiences:** Siloed asset management, manual processes, contractor dependence, political project selection and constant re-prioritisation.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Project optimism and blowouts:** Metro Tunnel cost grew from $10.9b to $13.5b; SRL East is under pressure. [A]
- **Maintenance backlog:** Road condition and the pothole backlog impose vehicle, safety and productivity costs. [B/C]
- **Fragmented agencies:** DTP, VIDA, SRLA, PTV and VicRoads duplicate corporate, planning and delivery functions.
- **Mode silos:** Bus, tram, train, road and active transport are planned and funded separately.
- **Fare and information fragmentation:** Multiple systems, poor integration and limited off-peak pricing.
- **Freight inefficiency:** Port, rail and road connections are not managed as one freight system.
- **Station precincts:** Transport projects are not consistently used to unlock housing and employment.
- **Contractor dependence:** The state has limited in-house delivery and asset management capability.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are maintenance backlogs, congestion, project variations and duplicated agencies. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Transport is a set of modes and projects; success is delivering the project.

**The new frame:** Transport is a **mobility service** and a **land-use system**; success is moving people and goods reliably at the lowest whole-of-life cost.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Project pipeline plus** | Keep building, add maintenance and assurance | Familiar; visible | Expensive; does not fix demand or integration | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. Mobility service** | One account, integrated fares, information, demand management, asset stewardship | Better service, lower cost | Requires data and governance | Preferred base |
| **C. Corridor authorities** | Integrated transport, housing and employment along corridors | Coordinated land use | Complex and slow | Use for priority corridors |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Maintain first, then expand.** A pothole repaired today is cheaper than a road rebuilt tomorrow. A reliable bus is faster to deliver than a rail line. The state should publish a whole-of-life asset plan and fund it before new mega-projects.

**Supporting moves:**

- **One transport account:** a single entitlement or payment system across modes, with concessions and off-peak pricing.
- **Mobility as a service:** integrated journey planning, ticketing and real-time information.
- **Asset stewardship:** a published road, rail and tram maintenance standard and backlog.
- **Project assurance:** an independent gate for cost, schedule, benefits and operating costs.
- **Congestion pricing pilot:** a fair, revenue-neutral trial in a corridor, with public transport alternatives.
- **Station precincts:** housing and employment around stations, with value capture.
- **Public maintenance fleet:** in-house rapid-response patching, as set out in the proposal.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate corporate and planning functions across transport agencies | DTP/VIDA/SRLA/PTV | Shared services; redeploy | Lower overhead | [D] |
| Pause or redesign low-benefit projects | DTP/VIDA | Redeploy delivery teams | Capital and operating | [D] |
| Reduce consultant and contractor reliance in planning and assurance | DTP | Build in-house capability | Consultant spend | [D] |
| Standardise fare and ticketing systems | PTV | One account, lower cost | Duplicated systems | [D] |
| Sunset low-value transport grants | DTP | Reallocate to maintenance | Program savings | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Pausing projects and consolidating agencies will be contested by contractors, unions and local communities. It is justified only with a transparent reallocation to maintenance, safety and service.
**What we will not cut:** Road safety; public transport services; maintenance; regional access.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-of-life asset plan and maintenance guarantee | Fix what we have | DTP/VicRoads/councils | Capital | Operating | Deferred rebuild costs |
| Public rapid-response patching fleet | Fast pothole repair | DTP/VicRoads | Capital | Operating | Contractor premiums |
| One transport account and integrated ticketing | Simpler, fairer travel | PTV | Capital | Operating | Fare systems |
| Project assurance unit | Stop blowouts | DTP/DTF | Low | Low | Variation and delay costs |
| Station precinct program | Housing and value capture | DTP/Development Victoria | Facilitated | Revenue | Land value |
| Freight corridor plan | Productivity and safety | DTP/ports/freight | Moderate | Operating | Freight costs |
| Active transport network | Health, congestion, emissions | Councils/DTP | Capital | Operating | Health and road costs |

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **manage assets, integrate services, assure projects and capture land value**, not just procure construction.

**The new deal:** The citizen gets a reliable journey and safer roads. The region gets connectivity. The state gets a lower whole-of-life cost and a credible project pipeline.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Infrastructure funding and reform | Better national productivity | State funds alone |
| Local government | Local roads, paths, planning | Maintenance funding, shared standards | Local network deteriorates |
| Contractors / unions | Productivity, skills, safety | Pipeline certainty, good jobs | Cost and delay |
| Transport operators | Service performance and data | Integrated system | Fragmentation |
| Freight / ports | Data and investment | Efficient corridors | Congestion and cost |
| Developers | Precinct delivery and value capture | Certainty and approvals | Disconnected growth |
| Passengers / communities | Feedback and patience | Better service | Trust falls |
| First Peoples | Cultural heritage and land | Self-determination | Conflict and delay |

**The convening mechanism:** A Transport and Land Use Board with DTP, VIDA, SRLA, PTV, councils, freight, operators and developers.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of road condition, potholes, service reliability, project cost/schedule and patronage.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Housing and planning** — transport and housing must be sequenced.
- **Regional Victoria** — connectivity and freight.
- **Local government** — local roads and paths.
- **Energy and climate** — electrification, emissions and resilience.

- **Procurement and market design** — local content and contractor capability.
- **Skills, jobs and industry** — construction and transport workforce.

**The shared asset:** A single transport, land-use and maintenance map.

## 10. Measures that matter

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Asset | Road condition | To be baselined | Up | DTP | Annual |
| Asset | Potholes repaired / backlog | To be baselined | Down | DTP | Monthly |
| Service | Public transport reliability | To be baselined | Up | PTV | Monthly |
| Demand | Patronage | To be baselined | Up | PTV | Monthly |
| Projects | Cost and schedule variance | Metro Tunnel +23%; SRL under pressure | Within approved | VAGO/DTP | Quarterly |
| Safety | Road fatalities and serious injuries | To be baselined | Down | TAC/Police | Monthly |
| Cost | Cost per passenger trip | To be baselined | Down | PTV | Annual |
| Access | Regional and outer-metro service | To be baselined | Up | PTV | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** Road condition and pothole backlog, because it is the most visible daily failure.
**The leading indicator:** Maintenance spend as a share of the asset base.
**The kill switch:** If project savings come from deferring maintenance or cutting safety, stop.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true maintenance backlog and whole-of-life cost.
- The benefits and costs of congestion pricing.
- The operating cost of the SRL and Metro Tunnel.
- The best model for integrated ticketing.
- The freight capacity constraints by corridor.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- An independent asset condition audit.
- A project assurance review of the major pipeline.
- A congestion pricing trial design.
- A freight corridor study.
