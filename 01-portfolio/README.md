# The Portfolio

**Sixteen citizen-facing policy areas, one connected system.**

A portfolio is not a silo. It is a **contract with the citizen** about a part of life the state can materially improve. The portfolio map below is deliberately outcome-shaped, not department-shaped. Several portfolios draw on the same departments, the same workforce, the same data and the same places. That is the point.

The cross-cutting levers live in [`../02-levers/`](../02-levers/README.md). They are the shared plumbing that makes the portfolios work: procurement, digital, workforce, regulation, federalism, integrity and the household budget.

## The joins that matter most

These are the dependencies that repeatedly break in government. Each one is a place where a portfolio cannot succeed alone.

| Join | Why it breaks | Portfolios involved |
|---|---|---|
| **Housing + transport + local government** | Homes are approved without the transport, schools, water and community infrastructure that make them work | 02, 07, 12, 15 |
| **Health + mental health + housing + aged care** | Patients cannot leave hospital without a home, a carer or a community service | 03, 04, 11 |
| **Skills + industry + regional development** | Training is funded without employers; employers cannot grow without skills and housing | 06, 12, 05 |
| **Energy + housing + transport + climate** | Electrification requires dwellings, grids, vehicles and consumer finance to move together | 08, 02, 07, 09 |
| **Justice + mental health + families + housing** | The same people cycle through police, courts, prisons, crisis care and homelessness | 10, 04, 11, 02 |
| **Emergency + climate + planning + local government** | Risk is managed by response agencies but created by land-use, insurance and infrastructure decisions | 13, 08, 02, 15 |
| **First Peoples + all portfolios** | Treaty, self-determination and truth-telling change how policy is made, not just what is funded | 14 + all |
| **Democracy + all portfolios** | Decisions are made without a public record, influence is undisclosed, and citizens cannot see the evidence or the trade-off | Democracy lever + all |
| **Fiscal + every portfolio** | Outcome budgets fail if the offsets and the operating costs are not real | 01 + all |

## The cut/add flows

The portfolio structure makes trade-offs visible. These are examples of value moving **between** areas, not just within them.

| Cut here | Add there | The logic |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicated corporate services across departments and agencies | Shared services, common platforms, frontline capability | Same output, lower cost, clearer accountability |
| Bespoke capital projects with weak assurance | Maintenance, renewal and digital assets | Avoid the gold-plating and the deferred maintenance bill |
| Activity-based contracts that pay for hours | Outcome contracts and place-based deals | Pay for results, not busyness |
| Universal concessions that subsidise the wealthy | Targeted entitlements plus price reform | Protect the people who need it, stop paying the people who do not |
| Fragmented agencies with overlapping mandates | One accountable owner for the citizen journey | Fewer doors, clearer responsibility |
| Road capacity for peak demand | Public transport, active transport and demand management | Move more people with less asphalt |
| Emergency response spend alone | Prevention, land-use planning and resilience | Reduce the need for response |
| Short-term grants to community organisations | Multi-year outcomes funding and capability | Stability, evaluation, less application churn |

## How to use the portfolio

1. **Start with the citizen promise.** If the portfolio cannot state one, it is not ready.
2. **Read the current machinery.** Know who is accountable before you propose to change it.
3. **Find the leak.** The money is usually in duplication, failure demand or a perverse incentive.
4. **Run the reframe.** At least three alternatives, including one that makes you uncomfortable.
5. **Complete the cut/add ledger.** No new money without an offset, a sunset or a measured return.
6. **Write the ecosystem deal.** Who gives, who gets, who objects.
7. **Check the joins.** Name at least three other portfolios or levers.
8. **Sequence for trust.** 100 days, 2 years, 10 years.
9. **Set the kill switch.** Say what would cause you to stop.
10. **Verify the evidence.** No orphan numbers.

## Where the evidence lives

Every portfolio has a matching folder in [`../raw/`](../raw/README.md). The raw folder holds the source files, a sidecar for each source and a manifest. Portfolio claims are mapped to sources in [`../04-evidence/source-register.md`](../04-evidence/source-register.md).

A portfolio README is the argument. A raw source is the evidence. A proposal in [`../03-proposals/`](../03-proposals/README.md) is the costed case. Do not confuse the three.

## The portfolio health check

A portfolio README is healthy when:

- The citizen promise is understandable without a glossary.
- The current machinery is described accurately, including the Commonwealth and local government.
- The leak is quantified, or the evidence gap is explicit.
- The reframe is genuinely different from the status quo.
- The cuts are specific enough to be contested.
- The additions have owners, costs and offsets.
- The ecosystem deal names the objectors.
- The joins are real dependencies, not rhetorical gestures.
- The first 100 days are doable.
- The measures include equity and integrity, not just efficiency.
- The kill switch is honest.
