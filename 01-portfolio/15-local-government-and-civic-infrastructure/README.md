# 15 — Local Government and Civic Infrastructure

> **Citizen promise:** Local decisions, fair rates, good places.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria's 79 councils are the state's most trusted delivery layer, but they are squeezed between rate caps, cost-shifting, ageing assets and unfunded mandates. The next move is to treat councils as **delivery partners, not cost-shift victims**: fix the funding model, aggregate back-office services, fund asset stewardship, and let councils collaborate at the scale the problem requires.
>
> **2026 baseline:** The standard council rate cap for 2026–27 is **2.75%**, with councils able to seek a higher cap from the Essential Services Commission. Glen Eira received approval for a **5%** cap. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Local Government, Consumer Affairs and Renters (Tim Richardson).
**Department(s):** Local Government Victoria (within DJSIR); Department of Treasury and Finance (rate cap and funding).
**Key agencies:** 79 councils; Municipal Association of Victoria (MAV); Victorian Local Governance Association (VLGA); Essential Services Commission (ESC); Victorian Auditor-General's Office (VAGO); IBAC; Victorian Grants Commission.
**Statute / framework:** Local Government Act 2020; Local Government Act 1989 (rate cap); Local Government (Rating) Act; Public Health and Wellbeing Act; Planning and Environment Act.
**Scale:** 79 councils; about 6,000 elected councillors and a large workforce; significant local infrastructure and services. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Councils are financially sustainable and accountable.
- **Outcome 2:** Local infrastructure is well maintained and fit for a growing population.
- **Outcome 3:** Rates and charges are fair, transparent and predictable.
- **Outcome 4:** Local democracy is healthy and trusted.
- **Outcome 5:** Councils collaborate at the scale needed for services, procurement and planning.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Fewer councils in financial distress; asset condition improves; rates are fair and stable; shared services reduce duplication; and community satisfaction rises.

**What failure looks like:** More rate caps, more cost-shifting, more asset failures, and councils cutting services or merging under duress.

**What the citizen experiences:** Rates bills that rise faster than wages, potholes, closed facilities, poor planning decisions and a council that is hard to influence.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Cost-shifting:** The state and Commonwealth assign responsibilities without adequate funding.
- **Rate cap squeeze:** Caps can protect ratepayers in the short term but constrain long-term asset stewardship.
- **Ageing assets:** Deferred maintenance and renewals create higher whole-of-life costs.
- **Small council capacity:** Many councils cannot sustain specialised functions or procurement scale.
- **Duplicated back office:** 79 councils duplicate finance, HR, IT, procurement and legal functions.
- **Grant dependence:** Short-term grants distort priorities and create administrative burden.
- **Planning capacity:** Under-resourced planning teams slow housing and infrastructure.
- **Integrity and governance:** Variation in standards, transparency and capability.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are deferred maintenance, duplicated back-office functions and cost-shifting. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Councils are separate governments with separate budgets, capped by the state.

**The new frame:** Councils are **place-based delivery partners** in a shared system, with a sustainable funding model, shared services, and a clear split of responsibilities.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Rate cap plus grants** | Keep the cap, increase grants | Familiar; protects ratepayers | Does not fix asset backlog | Not sufficient |
| **B. Sustainable local government** | Funding model reform, shared services, asset stewardship, performance framework | Durable and accountable | Requires state investment and trust | Preferred base |
| **C. Regional collaboration** | Councils share services, procurement and planning at regional scale | Scale and efficiency | Governance complexity | Strong complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Fund the responsibilities you assign.** A clear local government funding framework should match responsibilities, own-source revenue, grants and asset needs, with public reporting.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Shared services:** regional procurement, IT, HR, finance and legal services for smaller councils.
- **Asset stewardship:** a published asset condition and renewals plan for every council.
- **Rate reform:** a fair, transparent rate cap framework with a sustainability test and community engagement.
- **Regional collaboration:** joint planning, procurement and service delivery.
- **Integrity and capability:** common standards, training and support.
- **Community infrastructure:** fund the local infrastructure that makes housing and growth work.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicated back-office functions | Councils | Shared services; redeploy | Admin cost | [D] |
| Sunset low-value local grants and programs | State/councils | Reallocate to asset stewardship | Program savings | [D] |
| Stop assigning unfunded mandates | State | Clearer responsibilities | Cost-shifting | [D] |
| Consolidate small council procurement | Councils/MAV | Better prices | Procurement savings | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate reporting across state and local government | State/councils | Streamlined data | Admin time | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Rate reform and shared services will be contested by councils and ratepayers. They need genuine consultation and a clear evidence base.
**What we will not cut:** Local democracy; core services; asset maintenance; integrity.

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local government funding framework | Match responsibilities and revenue | DTF/LGV | Low | Operating | Cost-shifting |
| Shared services program | Scale for small councils | Councils/MAV | Moderate | Operating | Duplicated back office |
| Asset stewardship guarantee | Maintain local infrastructure | Councils/state | Capital | Operating | Deferred maintenance |
| Regional collaboration deals | Joint planning and procurement | Councils/regional boards | Low | Operating | Duplication |
| Integrity and capability program | Common standards | LGV/IBAC/VAGO | Low | Operating | Complaints and risk |
| Community infrastructure fund | Support growth | State/councils | Capital | Operating | Developer and rate pressure |

**The new capability:** The sector must be able to **share services, manage assets, plan for growth and demonstrate integrity**.

**The new deal:** The community gets good local services and fair rates. The council gets sustainable funding and support. The state gets a reliable delivery partner.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| State government | Fair funding and clear responsibilities | Local delivery and trust | Cost-shifting and blame |
| Commonwealth | Financial Assistance Grants | Local outcomes | Funding gaps |
| Councils | Accountability, asset management | Sustainable funding | Rate rises and service cuts |
| MAV / VLGA | Advocacy and support | Stronger sector | Fragmentation |
| ESC | Rate oversight | Fair outcomes | Political pressure |
| VAGO / IBAC | Audit and integrity | Public confidence | Scandal and distrust |
| Communities | Engagement and scrutiny | Better services | Apathy and conflict |
| Unions / workforce | Productivity and skills | Good jobs | Service disruption |

**The convening mechanism:** A Local Government Funding and Delivery Board with state, councils, MAV, VLGA, ESC, VAGO and IBAC.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public local government dashboard of financial sustainability, asset condition, rates, services and satisfaction.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Housing and planning** — councils approve housing and deliver local infrastructure.
- **Transport and roads** — local roads and paths.
- **Environment, water and circular economy** — waste and local environment.
- **Emergency management** — local response and recovery.
- **Regional Victoria** — regional collaboration and services.

**The kill switch:** If rate reform or shared services reduce local accountability, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The full cost of state and Commonwealth cost-shifting.
- The asset renewal gap by council.
- The savings from shared services.
- The distributional impact of rate reform.
- The capacity gaps in small and regional councils.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A cost-shifting audit.
- An asset condition audit.
- A shared services pilot.
- A rate reform review.
