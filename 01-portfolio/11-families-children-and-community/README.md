# 11 — Families, Children and Community

> **Citizen promise:** One family, one plan, one door.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria's family, child protection, family violence, disability, youth, carer and seniors services are separate systems that too often meet the same family multiple times without a shared plan. The next move is **one front door and one key worker**, with prevention and kinship care as the default, and the state purchasing outcomes rather than episodes.
>
> **2026 baseline:** The Victorian Housing Register had **57,604 new applicant households** in September 2026, including **17,262 homeless with support**. Average waits were about **17.2 months** for priority access and **18.6 months** for applicants prioritised because of family violence. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Children, Disability and Deputy Leader of the Government in the Legislative Council (Lizzie Blandthorn); Minister for Youth, Carers and Volunteers (Luba Grigorovitch); Minister for Housing, Building and Precincts, Homelessness and Seniors (Nick Staikos); Minister for Prevention of Family Violence and Attorney-General (Sonya Kilkenny).
**Department(s):** Department of Families, Fairness and Housing (DFFH).
**Key agencies:** Child Protection; The Orange Door; family violence services; disability services; kinship and out-of-home care; youth services; Seniors Victoria; carers support; community service organisations; NDIS (Commonwealth).
**Statute / framework:** Children, Youth and Families Act; Family Violence Protection Act; Disability Act; Carers Recognition Act; National Disability Insurance Scheme Act; Child Safe Standards.
**Scale:** One of the largest community service systems in the state, with significant contracted delivery. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Children are safe, and families get help before crisis.
- **Outcome 2:** Family violence victims are protected and supported.
- **Outcome 3:** People with disability can live the life they choose.
- **Outcome 4:** Carers, seniors and young people are supported and included.
- **Outcome 5:** Community services are integrated around the family, not the program.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Fewer children entering out-of-home care; more kinship care; faster family violence support; better disability outcomes; carers feeling supported; and families telling a coherent story once, not many times.

**What failure looks like:** More reports, more assessments, more placements, more hand-offs and the same families in crisis.

## 2. Current machinery

| Layer | Who / what | Role | Spend or scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Children | DFFH / Child Protection | Safety and care | Large |
| Family violence | DFFH / Orange Door / services | Support and safety | Large |
| Disability | DFFH / NDIS interface | Services and coordination | Mixed |
| Kinship / care | DFFH / community providers | Placement and support | Large |
| Youth | DFFH / youth services | Support and participation | Statewide |
| Seniors / carers | DFFH / community | Support and inclusion | Statewide |
| Community | NGOs / councils / health | Delivery | Contracted |
| Commonwealth | NDIS, family payments, aged care | National | National |

**What the citizen experiences:** They tell their story repeatedly. They are assessed by different agencies against different thresholds. They may be told they are "not eligible" for one service and "too complex" for another. Crisis comes before help.
**What the frontline experiences:** High caseloads, risk aversion, administrative burden, short funding cycles and a system that punishes the wrong things.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Multiple assessments:** The same family is assessed by child protection, family services, family violence, housing, mental health and disability.
- **Out-of-home care costs:** Placement and support costs are high; kinship care is under-supported.
- **Family violence demand:** Waitlists and housing shortages leave victims at risk.
- **Disability interface:** NDIS and state services do not always coordinate, leaving gaps.
- **Carer and senior support:** Fragmented programs and low take-up.
- **Youth services:** Disconnected from education, housing and mental health.
- **Contracted complexity:** Many small contracts, high reporting burden and limited outcome measurement.
- **Data gaps:** No shared view of the family or the outcome.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest costs are out-of-home care, crisis accommodation, family violence, hospital and justice contact. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Each program has its own eligibility, assessment, funding and reporting.

**The new frame:** The family has **one plan, one key worker and one front door**, with pooled funding and shared outcomes.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More programs and places** | Expand each service stream | Meets demand | Does not fix fragmentation | Necessary but not sufficient |
| **B. One family, one plan** | Integrated intake, key worker, pooled funding, shared data | Better experience and outcomes | Requires trust and governance | Preferred base |
| **C. Community-led family hubs** | Local hubs with co-located services and community governance | Prevention and trust | Coverage and consistency | Strong local model |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Prevention and kinship first.** Invest in family support, early intervention and kinship care before removing children; and support kinship carers properly.

**Supporting moves:**

- **One front door:** The Orange Door as the single entry point for family violence, child and family services, with no wrong door.
- **One key worker:** A named person who coordinates the plan and stays with the family.
- **Pooled funding:** A single budget for a family's package, with outcomes rather than program rules.
- **Kinship first:** Support kinship carers with finance, housing, respite and legal assistance.
- **Disability interface:** A single state-NDIS coordination function.
- **Carer and senior strategy:** One access point, one assessment, one plan.
- **Outcome contracts:** Pay providers for safety, stability and wellbeing.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate assessments and intake processes | DFFH/community | One assessment, shared | Admin time | [D] |
| Reduce out-of-home care where safe kinship care is available | DFFH | Kinship support and finance | Placement cost | [D] |
| Sunset low-value community programs without outcomes | DFFH | Reallocate to prevention | Program savings | [D] |
| Consolidate small contracts and reporting | DFFH | Fewer, larger outcome contracts | Admin cost | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate disability coordination | DFFH/NDIS | One plan | Admin cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Child protection and out-of-home care reform are high-risk. The state must guarantee child safety and support the workforce through the transition.
**What we will not cut:** Child safety; family violence crisis response; disability support; kinship support.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Integrated family front door | One plan, one key worker | DFFH/Orange Door | Moderate | Operating | Duplicated assessments |
| Kinship care support package | Keep children with family | DFFH/community | Moderate | Operating | Out-of-home care |
| Family violence housing partnership | Safety and stability | DFFH/Homes Victoria | Capital | Operating | Crisis accommodation |
| Disability-NDIS coordination | Close the gaps | DFFH/NDIS | Low | Operating | Duplicated services |
| Carer and senior one-stop shop | Simpler access | DFFH/community | Low | Operating | Fragmented programs |
| Youth transition support | Housing, work, education | DFFH/DJSIR | Moderate | Operating | Justice and homelessness |
| Outcome-based community contracts | Pay for results | DFFH/community | Low | Operating | Program churn |

**The new capability:** The system must be able to **integrate intake, pool funding, share data and commission outcomes**.

**The new deal:** The family gets one plan and one person. The community provider gets stable funding. The state gets better outcomes and lower crisis costs.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | NDIS, family payments, aged care | Better coordination | State fills gaps |
| Local government | Community infrastructure and local knowledge | Funding and partnership | Fragmentation |
| Community organisations | Trust, reach, delivery | Multi-year contracts | Service gaps |
| Courts / police | Family violence and child protection interface | Better outcomes | Justice and child protection costs |
| Health / mental health | Treatment and support | Integrated care | Crisis escalation |
| Housing | Stable homes | Better family outcomes | Homelessness |
| First Peoples | Cultural authority | Self-determination | Culturally unsafe care |
| Families / young people | Engagement and feedback | Better services | Trust falls |

**The convening mechanism:** A Family and Community Services Board with DFFH, Orange Door, community providers, health, housing, justice, education and First Peoples.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of child safety, out-of-home care, family violence, housing, disability and carer outcomes.

---

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **Housing and planning** — family violence and homelessness.
- **Health and mental health** — integrated care and crisis response.
- **Justice, police and community safety** — family violence and youth justice.
- **Education and early childhood** — early intervention and school support.
- **First Peoples and multicultural affairs** — self-determination and cultural safety.
- **Workforce and capability** — community sector workforce.
- **Digital, data and AI** — shared family record and consent.

**The shared asset:** A consent-based family plan and record across services.

## 10. Measures that matter

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Children | Children in out-of-home care | To be baselined | Down | DFFH | Quarterly |
| Kinship | Kinship placements | To be baselined | Up | DFFH | Quarterly |
| Family violence | Wait for support | To be baselined | Down | DFFH | Quarterly |
| Housing | Stable housing at exit | To be baselined | Up | DFFH | Quarterly |
| Disability | NDIS coordination | To be baselined | Up | DFFH | Annual |
| Carers | Carer wellbeing | To be baselined | Up | DFFH | Annual |
| Experience | Family experience of integration | To be baselined | Up | Survey | Annual |
| Safety | Repeat child protection reports | To be baselined | Down | DFFH | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** The number of children in out-of-home care, because it measures whether prevention and kinship are working.
**The leading indicator:** The share of families with a single plan and key worker.
**The kill switch:** If integration reduces child safety or family violence support, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The cost of duplicate assessments across the family service system.
- The number of kinship carers and their support needs.
- The family violence demand and housing gap.
- The NDIS-state interface gaps.
- The outcomes of community service contracts.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A family service journey map.
- A kinship care census.
- A family violence housing trial.
- An outcome contract pilot.
