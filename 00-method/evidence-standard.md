# Evidence Standard

**No orphan numbers. No undated facts. No confident guesses.**

This repository is a policy library, not a campaign pamphlet. Its credibility depends on the discipline of its evidence. Every number, claim and comparison must be traceable, date-stamped and labelled.

---

## 1. Source hierarchy

Use the highest available source. Move down only when the higher source does not exist.

| Tier | Source type | Examples | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Primary official | Legislation, budget papers, annual reports, VAGO reports, ABS data, court judgments, treaties, intergovernmental agreements | Best evidence for facts, baselines and statutory constraints |
| **2** | Official evaluation / regulator | Program evaluations, productivity commissions, auditor reports, regulator determinations, parliamentary inquiries | Best evidence for what works and what it costs |
| **3** | Peer-reviewed / expert | Academic research, independent think tanks with transparent methods, professional bodies | Best evidence for mechanism and international comparison |
| **4** | Reputable media / industry | Major newspapers, industry associations, unions, advocacy groups | Useful for direction, timing and stakeholder position; corroborate before using as fact |
| **5** | Anecdote / claim / assumption | Individual case, stakeholder assertion, social media, AI-generated summary | Treat as a hypothesis, never as evidence |

**Rule:** a Tier 4 or 5 source can tell you what someone believes. It cannot tell you what is true. Label it accordingly.

---

## 2. Confidence labels

Apply a label to every material number or claim.

| Label | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| **[A]** | Primary, current, independently auditable | Can a sceptical reader click through to the number and see the same thing? |
| **[B]** | Official, but dated, partial or not independently audited | Is the vintage clear, and is the limitation stated? |
| **[C]** | Stakeholder, industry or media claim | Is it corroborated by a Tier 1–3 source, or clearly presented as a claim? |
| **[D]** | Assumption, estimate or projection | Is the method stated, and is there a sensitivity range? |

If a number has no label, treat it as **[D]**.

---

## 3. Numeric discipline

For every number, record:

- **Value and unit** — dollars, people, dwellings, tonnes, percentage.
- **Vintage** — the period the number describes, not the date it was published.
- **Geography** — Victoria, a region, a city, a service catchment.
- **Definition** — FTE vs headcount, operating vs capital, gross vs net, nominal vs real.
- **Source** — publisher, title, page or table, URL, access date.
- **Confidence** — A/B/C/D.
- **Direction** — is it a cost, a saving, a transfer, an avoided cost or a benefit?

**Never add numbers with different definitions.** A consultant FTE is not a contractor headcount. A capital commitment is not an annual operating cost. A gross saving is not a net saving.

---

## 4. Separate fact, estimate, assumption and judgment

Use explicit language:

- **Fact:** "The 2026–27 Pre-Election Budget Update forecasts net debt of $173.1 billion at 30 June 2027." [A]
- **Estimate:** "The fleet's annual output is estimated at 380,000–665,000 patches." [D]
- **Assumption:** "We assume 190 workable dry days per year." [D]
- **Judgment:** "This is likely to be politically contested because it reduces executive roles." [Judgment]

Do not dress a judgment as a fact. Do not dress an assumption as an estimate.

---

## 5. Contradiction log

When sources disagree, do not average them. Record the disagreement:

| Claim | Source A | Source B | Why they differ | What we will use | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | | |

Common reasons for disagreement: different definitions, different vintages, different geographies, gross vs net, optimistic vs conservative assumptions, stakeholder advocacy. The contradiction is often the most useful part of the evidence.

---

## 6. AI-generated and secondary summaries

Web search summaries, AI-generated answers and secondary explainers are **navigation tools, not sources**. They can point you to a primary source; they cannot replace it.

Rule: if a claim matters, open the primary source and cite it directly. If you cannot access the primary source, label the claim [C] or [D] and say so.

---

## 7. Data ethics and privacy

- Do not publish identifiable personal information.
- Do not aggregate small populations in a way that re-identifies individuals.
- Respect data sovereignty, especially for First Peoples' data.
- State the limitations of administrative data: who is missing, who is misclassified, who is not counted.
- Prefer public, reproducible data. If you use confidential data, describe the access and the constraints without exposing it.

---

## 8. Update cadence

| Document | Review trigger |
|---|---|
| Portfolio README | Every budget, every election cycle, or when a major audit/evaluation lands |
| Source register | Monthly during an active policy development period |
| Costed proposal | Before publication, and after any material change in assumptions |
| Fiscal figures | Every budget update and Pre-Election Budget Update |

Every update should record what changed and why in the document or the source register.

---

## 9. Publication checklist

Before a policy area is published or taken to Cabinet:

- [ ] Every material number has a source and a confidence label.
- [ ] Every estimate states its method and range.
- [ ] Every statutory constraint is identified.
- [ ] Every cross-portfolio dependency is named.
- [ ] Every stakeholder who must move is named.
- [ ] The counterfactual is stated.
- [ ] The pre-mortem has been run.
- [ ] The source register is updated.
- [ ] The document says what we will stop doing.
- [ ] The document says when we will stop the policy if it fails.

If you cannot tick every box, the policy is a draft, not a decision.
