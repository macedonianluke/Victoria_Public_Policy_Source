# Digital, Data and AI

> **Citizen promise:** Tell the government once, and get an answer that makes sense.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria has good digital strategies and a growing AI agenda, but the citizen still experiences 306 agencies as 306 doors. The next move is **once-only government**: common identity, payments, notifications and data, with AI used under clear assurance and human accountability.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria released an **AI Mission Statement on 30 January 2026** with six pillars: investment and adoption, talent, data centres and digital infrastructure, local AI innovation, ethical AI, and public-sector adoption. The Department of Government Services launched **VicGov Chat** for public servants in early 2026. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Government Services and Finance (Enver Erdogan); Minister for AI and Digital Economy, Major Events, Medical Research, Racing and Economic Development (Anthony Carbines).
**Department(s):** Department of Government Services (DGS); Department of Premier and Cabinet (DPC); Department of Jobs, Skills, Industry and Regions (DJSIR).
**Key agencies:** DGS digital and cyber teams; VicGov Chat; Service Victoria; Victorian Centre for Data Insights; AI Advisory Committee; privacy and integrity regulators.
**Statute / framework:** Privacy and Data Protection Act; Public Records Act; Charter of Human Rights; AI Mission Statement; Victorian Digital Strategy 2021–26; cyber strategy.
**Scale:** Whole-of-government digital, data, cyber and AI investment. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Citizens and businesses tell government once.
- **Outcome 2:** Services are simple, accessible and reliable.
- **Outcome 3:** Data is shared safely to improve decisions and outcomes.
- **Outcome 4:** AI is used responsibly, with human accountability and public trust.
- **Outcome 5:** The public service has the digital capability to commission and manage technology.

**What success looks like by 2030:** A single citizen/business identity; common payments and notifications; reusable platforms; faster service delivery; fewer data breaches; and public confidence in AI use.

**What failure looks like:** More apps, more portals, more vendors, more AI pilots, and the same fragmented experience.

**What the citizen experiences:** Multiple logins, repeated information, paper forms, long waits, inconsistent answers and services that do not talk to each other.
**What the frontline experiences:** Duplicated systems, manual workarounds, poor data, vendor lock-in and slow change.

---

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Duplicated platforms:** Agencies buy or build the same identity, payments, notifications and case management.
- **Legacy systems:** High maintenance costs and limited integration.
- **Data silos:** Data is not shared safely, so citizens repeat themselves and policy is blind.
- **Vendor lock-in:** Long contracts, proprietary systems and weak exit rights.
- **AI pilots:** Enthusiasm without assurance, evaluation or scaling.
- **Cyber risk:** Growing threat surface and uneven capability.
- **Digital exclusion:** People without devices, data or confidence are left behind.
- **Capability gap:** The public service cannot commission or manage complex technology.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. Duplicated platforms and legacy maintenance are likely the largest items. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Each agency digitises its own services.

**The new frame:** Government builds **common platforms once** and lets agencies compose services on top.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Agency-by-agency** | Each agency buys its own systems | Autonomy | Duplication and fragmentation | Status quo |
| **B. Common platforms** | Identity, payments, notifications, data, case management as shared utilities | Reuse, lower cost, better experience | Governance and funding | Preferred base |
| **C. Platform plus AI** | Common platforms with assured AI services and human oversight | Scale and innovation | Trust and risk | Long-term direction |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Once-only government.** A citizen or business should provide information once, and government should reuse it lawfully across services.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Common identity:** one login for all government services, with privacy and consent.
- **Common payments and notifications:** one system, many services.
- **Data sharing infrastructure:** lawful, secure, auditable.
- **AI assurance framework:** risk assessment, transparency, human review, evaluation and redress.
- **Digital inclusion:** devices, data, skills and assisted channels.
- **Public sector capability:** product management, data engineering, cyber and contract management.
- **Open data and transparency:** publish what can be published.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate platforms and licences | DGS/agencies | Shared platforms; redeploy | Licence and maintenance | [D] |
| Stop AI pilots without assurance and evaluation | DGS/agencies | Focus on proven use cases | Pilot spend | [D] |
| Reduce legacy system duplication | DGS/agencies | Migration support | Maintenance cost | [D] |
| Sunset low-value digital grants and apps | DGS | Reallocate to platforms | Program savings | [D] |
| Reduce vendor lock-in through exit rights | DGS | Better competition | Contract risk | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Consolidating platforms and retiring legacy systems will disrupt agencies and vendors. It requires a funded migration and a service guarantee.
**What we will not cut:** Cyber security; privacy; assisted channels; essential services.

## 6. Add here

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common identity and consent | Once-only | DGS/Service Victoria | Capital | Operating | Duplicated logins |
| Common payments and notifications | Reuse | DGS | Capital | Operating | Duplicated systems |
| Data sharing platform | Lawful reuse | DGS/VCDI | Capital | Operating | Data silos |
| AI assurance framework | Trust and safety | DGS/DJSIR | Low | Operating | Pilot risk |
| Digital inclusion program | Access | DGS/community | Moderate | Operating | Exclusion costs |
| Public sector digital academy | Capability | DGS/VPSC | Low | Operating | Consultant reliance |
| Open data program | Transparency | DGS/agencies | Low | Operating | Existing data |

**The new capability:** The public service must be able to **design services, manage platforms, govern data and assure AI**.

**The new deal:** The citizen gets simpler services and control over their data. The public servant gets better tools. The state gets reuse, lower cost and trust.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Interoperability and data standards | Better national services | Duplication |
| Vendors | Open standards and exit rights | Fair competition | Lock-in |
| Citizens | Consent and feedback | Simpler services | Distrust |
| Privacy / integrity regulators | Guidance and oversight | Compliance and trust | Risk |
| Councils | Shared platforms | Lower cost | Fragmentation |
| Universities / TAFEs | Skills and research | Talent pipeline | Capability gap |
| Community organisations | Assisted channels | Inclusion | Exclusion |

**The convening mechanism:** A Digital and Data Board with DGS, agencies, privacy regulators, vendors, councils and community.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public digital dashboard of service performance, reuse, data sharing, AI use, cyber incidents and inclusion.

## 8. Cross-portfolio joins

- **All portfolios** — every service is delivered through digital and data.
- **Fiscal and public sector** — shared platforms and back-office savings.
- **Procurement and market design** — vendor management and open standards.
- **Workforce and capability** — digital skills.
- **Integrity and trust** — privacy, transparency and AI accountability.
- **Intergovernmental and federalism** — national interoperability.

**The shared asset:** Common identity, payments, notifications, data and AI assurance.

**The kill switch:** If AI use cannot be explained, audited or appealed, stop.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The whole-of-government IT spend and duplication.
- The number of AI use cases and their risk.
- The cost of digital exclusion.
- The best model for common identity.
- The data sharing barriers across agencies.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- An IT spend and duplication audit.
- An AI assurance pilot.
- A common identity trial.
- A digital inclusion evaluation.
