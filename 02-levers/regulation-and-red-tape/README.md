# Regulation and Red Tape

> **Citizen promise:** The rules are clear, fair and fast — and they protect what matters.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria regulates through many agencies, Acts, permits, codes and reporting requirements. The burden is real, but the answer is not "cut regulation". It is **regulatory stewardship**: measure the burden, remove duplication, regulate by risk, digitise approvals, and review every rule on a sunset cycle.
>
> **2026 baseline:** The planning system is being reformed through the *Planning Amendment (Better Decisions Made Faster) Act 2026*, with staged commencement into 2027. Local Jobs First reforms commenced on **1 July 2026**. [A]

**Minister(s):** Portfolio ministers; Minister for Government Services and Finance (Enver Erdogan); Minister for Local Government and Consumer Affairs (Tim Richardson).
**Department(s):** Department of Government Services (DGS); Department of Treasury and Finance (DTF); portfolio departments.
**Key agencies:** EPA; WorkSafe; Consumer Affairs Victoria; Building and Plumbing Commission; Victorian Building Authority; VCAT; local councils; Ombudsman; VAGO.
**Statute / framework:** Subordinate Legislation Act; Environment Protection Act; Occupational Health and Safety Act; Building Act; Planning and Environment Act; Consumer Affairs legislation; Freedom of Information Act.
**Scale:** Hundreds of regulators, permits, codes and reporting requirements across the economy. [D]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Rules achieve their purpose at the lowest reasonable cost.
- **Outcome 2:** Approvals are fast, predictable and digital.
- **Outcome 3:** Regulators are consistent, risk-based and accountable.
- **Outcome 4:** Business and community can comply without unnecessary effort.
- **Outcome 5:** Regulation is reviewed, renewed or removed on a fixed cycle.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Lower compliance cost; faster approvals; fewer duplicate regulators; fewer complaints; and better safety, environmental and consumer outcomes.

**What failure looks like:** More rules, more forms, more regulators and the same problems.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Duplicate approvals:** Multiple agencies and councils regulate the same activity.
- **Manual processes:** Paper forms, repeated information and slow decisions.
- **Inconsistent advice:** Different regulators interpret the same rule differently.
- **Outdated rules:** Legacy requirements that no longer serve a purpose.
- **Compliance burden:** Small business and community organisations bear disproportionate cost.
- **Weak measurement:** The state does not know the total cost of its rules.
- **Enforcement gaps:** Poor compliance and uneven penalties.
- **Regulatory capture:** Close relationships between regulators and regulated.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The burden falls on business, households, councils and frontline services. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Regulation is a set of rules to be administered.

**The new frame:** Regulation is a **system to be stewarded**: measure the burden, design for compliance, digitise, enforce proportionately and review on a cycle.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Deregulation** | Cut rules and staff | Quick relief | Risk and public harm | Not sufficient |
| **B. Regulatory stewardship** | Burden measurement, risk-based rules, digital permits, sunset reviews | Durable and safe | Requires capability | Preferred base |
| **C. Co-regulation** | Industry and community share responsibility | Flexibility | Capture and uneven enforcement | Use selectively |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Regulatory burden is a tax.** Measure it, publish it, and require every new rule to be offset by a removed or simplified rule.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Regulatory burden dashboard:** time and cost to comply, by rule and regulator.
- **One-stop digital permits:** one application, shared data, tracked decision.
- **Risk-based regulation:** focus enforcement on harm, not paperwork.
- **Sunset and review:** every rule reviewed on a fixed cycle.
- **Regulatory sandboxes:** test new models with safeguards.
- **Mutual recognition:** accept other jurisdictions' approvals where safe.
- **Regulator performance standards:** timeliness, consistency and transparency.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Remove duplicate approvals across agencies and councils | DGS/regulators | One approval | Time and cost | [D] |
| Sunset outdated rules and reporting | DGS/regulators | Fewer forms | Compliance cost | [D] |
| Consolidate overlapping regulators | DGS/portfolio | Shared functions | Overhead | [D] |
| Reduce low-value compliance reporting | Regulators | Risk-based reporting | Business time | [D] |
| Stop gold-plating local standards where national standards exist | Councils/state | Consistency | Cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Removing rules can reduce protection. Each removal must be evidence-based and consult the community.
**What we will not cut:** Safety; environmental protection; consumer protection; integrity.

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |

| Business / community | Compliance and feedback | Lower burden | Enforcement and cost |
| Regulators | Risk-based practice and data | Clear standards | Inconsistency |

| Commonwealth | National standards and mutual recognition | Better outcomes | Duplication |
| Courts / tribunals | Timely review | Clear rules | Backlogs |
| Ombudsman / VAGO | Oversight | Integrity | Complaints |
| Citizens | Engagement | Protection | Harm |

**The convening mechanism:** A Regulatory Reform Board with DGS, DTF, regulators, business, community, councils and Commonwealth.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public dashboard of compliance cost, approval times, regulator performance and complaints.

| Digital | Permits online end-to-end | To be baselined | Up | DGS | Annual |
| Safety | Harm outcomes | To be baselined | Stable/up | regulators | Annual |
| Trust | Business and community confidence | To be baselined | Up | Survey | Annual |
| Integrity | Regulatory capture complaints | To be baselined | Down | IBAC | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** The cost of compliance for a small business or community organisation.
**The leading indicator:** The number of permits available end-to-end online.
**The kill switch:** If burden falls but harm rises, stop and redesign.

---

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The total compliance cost by rule and sector.
- The number of regulators and approvals.
- The time and cost of each approval.
- The consistency of regulatory decisions.
- The effect of reform on safety and environmental outcomes.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A regulatory burden census.
- A permits digital pilot.
- A sandbox trial.
- A regulator performance review.
