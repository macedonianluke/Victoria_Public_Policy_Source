# Integrity and Trust

> **Citizen promise:** You can see what your government is doing, and you can hold it to account.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Trust is not a communications problem. It is an institutional design problem. Victoria has strong integrity bodies, but the citizen still experiences secrecy, lobbying, donations, procurement opacity and slow accountability. The next move is **open by default, integrity by design, and citizen voice by default**.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria has an independent IBAC, VAGO and Ombudsman, a Freedom of Information regime, a lobbyist register and a public appointments process. The question is implementation, resourcing and follow-through, not the absence of institutions. [B]

**Minister(s):** Premier (Ben Carroll); Special Minister of State (Ingrid Stitt); Attorney-General (Sonya Kilkenny).
**Department(s):** Department of Premier and Cabinet (DPC); Department of Justice and Community Safety (DJCS).
**Key agencies:** IBAC; VAGO; Victorian Ombudsman; Office of the Victorian Information Commissioner (OVIC); Victorian Electoral Commission; Parliament; courts; Auditor-General; public sector boards.
**Statute / framework:** IBAC Act; Audit Act; Ombudsman Act; Freedom of Information Act; Public Administration Act; Electoral Act; Local Government Act; lobbying and donations laws.
**Scale:** Whole-of-government integrity system. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Citizens can see how decisions are made and who influences them.
- **Outcome 2:** Corruption, waste and misconduct are detected and addressed.
- **Outcome 3:** Public appointments and procurement are merit-based and transparent.
- **Outcome 4:** Citizens have a real voice between elections.
- **Outcome 5:** Institutions are trusted and legitimate.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Higher trust; faster FOI; more open data; stronger lobbying and donations transparency; fewer repeat audit findings; and citizen assemblies that influence decisions.

**What failure looks like:** More scandals, more reviews, more apologies, and declining trust.

**What the citizen experiences:** They cannot find out why a decision was made, who lobbied for it, or what happened to the audit recommendation. FOI is slow and expensive.
**What the frontline experiences:** Risk aversion, unclear rules, and a fear of speaking up.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Secrecy:** Decisions are made without a public record.
- **Lobbying:** Influence is not transparent.
- **Donations:** Money in politics is not transparent enough.
- **Procurement:** Contract awards and variations are hard to scrutinise.
- **Public appointments:** Boards and agencies are not consistently merit-based.
- **FOI delays:** Requests are slow, costly and sometimes refused without clear reasons.
- **Audit follow-up:** Recommendations are not consistently implemented.
- **Whistleblowers:** Protections are weak and retaliation is feared.
- **Citizen voice:** Consultation is often tokenistic.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. Corruption and waste are hard to measure, but the cost of distrust is real. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Integrity is a set of rules and watchdogs.

**The new frame:** Trust is **infrastructure**: open data, transparent influence, accountable appointments, protected whistleblowers and real citizen voice.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More rules** | New codes and registers | Visible | Compliance without culture | Not sufficient |
| **B. Open by default** | Publish decisions, contracts, lobbying, data | Trust and scrutiny | Privacy and commercial risk | Preferred base |
| **C. Citizen voice** | Assemblies, participatory budgeting, co-design | Legitimacy | Time and representativeness | Strong complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Open by default.** If a document, contract, decision or data set can be published safely, it should be — without an FOI request.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Open data and decision registers:** publish cabinet decisions, contracts, grants, appointments and lobbying.
- **Lobbying register reform:** real-time, searchable, with meetings disclosed.
- **Donations reform:** lower thresholds, real-time disclosure and caps.
- **Public appointments:** merit-based, published and independently assessed.
- **Whistleblower protection:** independent support and anti-retaliation.
- **Citizen assemblies:** random, deliberative and influential.
- **Audit follow-up:** a public tracker of recommendations and implementation.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reduce low-value PR and advertising | DPC/agencies | Reallocate to services | Spend | [D] |
| Sunset outdated secrecy provisions | DPC/agencies | Open information | Trust | [D] |
| Consolidate duplicate integrity reporting | DPC/agencies | One portal | Admin cost | [D] |
| Stop uncompetitive public appointments | DPC/boards | Merit-based process | Trust | [D] |
| Reduce FOI delays through proactive release | OVIC/agencies | Faster access | Admin cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Opening up lobbying, donations and appointments will be resisted by established interests. It is the price of trust.
**What we will not cut:** Independent integrity bodies; privacy; national security protections.

| Public servants | Speak up | Protection | Misconduct |

| Political parties | Donations transparency | Trust | Cynicism |

**The convening mechanism:** A Integrity and Trust Council with Parliament, integrity bodies, civil society, media, business and citizens.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public trust dashboard of FOI, lobbying, donations, appointments, audit follow-up, complaints and trust.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true cost of FOI delay and secrecy.
- The extent of lobbying and donations influence.
- The implementation rate of audit recommendations.
- The best model for citizen assemblies.
- The trust levels of different communities.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A trust survey.
- An FOI audit.
- A lobbying and donations review.
- A citizen assembly pilot.
