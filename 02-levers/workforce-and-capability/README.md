# Workforce and Capability

> **Citizen promise:** The people who deliver your services are there, skilled and supported.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Almost every policy failure eventually becomes a workforce failure: too few teachers, nurses, carers, planners, electricians, drivers, planners and contract managers. Victoria plans workforce in fragments. The next move is to treat **workforce as infrastructure**: forecast demand, fund the pipeline, fix retention, and use migration, housing and regional incentives deliberately.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria projects a shortfall of about **2,661 secondary teachers** in 2026, and about **196,000 new workers** are expected in occupations typically requiring VET qualifications over the next three years. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Skills and Training, Education and First Peoples (Gabrielle Williams); Minister for Jobs and Industry (Melissa Horne); Minister for Health (Ingrid Stitt); Minister for Finance and Government Services (Enver Erdogan).
**Department(s):** Department of Jobs, Skills, Industry and Regions (DJSIR); Department of Education (DE); Department of Health (DH); Victorian Public Sector Commission (VPSC).
**Key agencies:** Victorian Skills Authority; TAFEs; universities; Jobs Victoria; professional registration boards; industry bodies; unions; councils.
**Statute / framework:** Education and Training Reform Act; Health Practitioner Regulation National Law; migration law (Commonwealth); public sector employment framework.
**Scale:** Whole-of-state workforce, including public sector, care economy, construction, energy and transport. [B]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Critical roles are filled, in the right places.
- **Outcome 2:** The workforce has the skills the economy needs.
- **Outcome 3:** Retention improves: people stay, progress and are safe.
- **Outcome 4:** Regional and disadvantaged communities have fair access.
- **Outcome 5:** The public service can commission, manage and deliver.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Vacancies fall in priority occupations; completion and retention rise; regional gaps narrow; and the public service has the capability to deliver the reform agenda.

**What failure looks like:** More recruitment campaigns, more migration, more training places, but the same shortages and turnover.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Fragmented forecasting:** Each portfolio forecasts separately, so the state misses aggregate demand.
- **Training mismatch:** Places are not consistently aligned to vacancies and completions.
- **Retention failure:** Turnover and burnout cost more than recruitment.
- **Regional gaps:** Housing, transport and spousal employment make regional roles hard to fill.
- **Migration friction:** Skills recognition, settlement and housing are not coordinated.
- **Agency reliance:** Health, education and construction use expensive temporary labour.
- **Public sector capability:** Contract management, digital, planning and evaluation skills are thin.
- **Care economy:** Low pay, insecurity and undervaluation.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The largest items are turnover, agency premiums, unfilled vacancies and delayed projects. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Each portfolio recruits and trains its own workforce.

**The new frame:** The state plans **one workforce system** by occupation, region and industry, with shared forecasting, funding and migration.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More recruitment** | Advertise, incentivise, recruit overseas | Quick | Does not fix retention or pipeline | Not sufficient |
| **B. Workforce infrastructure** | Forecast, fund pipeline, retention, migration, housing | Durable | Requires coordination | Preferred base |
| **C. Care economy compact** | Raise pay, security and conditions in care | Quality and retention | Fiscal cost | Necessary for care |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Workforce is infrastructure.** Plan it like a road: forecast demand, fund the pipeline, maintain it, and measure the outcome.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Statewide workforce forecast:** by occupation, region and industry, updated annually.
- **Pipeline funding:** tied to completions and job outcomes.
- **Retention package:** pay, conditions, rostering, career paths and safety.
- **Regional workforce deal:** housing, transport, childcare and spousal employment.
- **Migration and settlement:** skills recognition, housing and community support.
- **Public sector capability:** contract management, digital, planning and evaluation.
- **Care economy compact:** wages, security and quality.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate duplicate workforce programs | DJSIR/DE/DH | One plan per occupation | Admin cost | [D] |
| Reduce agency and locum reliance | Health/education | Permanent recruitment | Agency premium | [D] |
| Sunset low-value training subsidies | DJSIR | Reallocate to priority skills | Program savings | [D] |
| Reduce duplicated registration and credential checks | Regulators | Mutual recognition | Admin time | [D] |
| Consolidate small workforce grants | DJSIR | Reallocate to retention | Program savings | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Retention reform and care wages cost money. They should be funded by reducing turnover, agency reliance and low-value programs.
**What we will not cut:** Safety; registration standards; training quality; regional access.

| Regional workforce deal | Housing, transport, childcare | RDV/DFFH/DJSIR | Moderate | Operating | Vacancies |

**The new capability:** The state must be able to **forecast, fund, recruit, retain and deploy workforce across portfolios**.

**The new deal:** The worker gets a good job and a career. The employer gets a reliable pipeline. The citizen gets the service.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Migration, skills funding, universities | Better outcomes | State fills gaps |
| Universities / TAFEs | Places and quality | Funding and planning | Shortages |
| Employers | Jobs, training, conditions | Skilled workforce | Turnover |
| Unions | Reform engagement | Pay, safety, careers | Industrial action |
| Councils | Housing, childcare, local services | Workforce | Regional decline |
| Industry | Demand data | Pipeline | Vacancies |
| Regions | Place-based coordination | Workers | Decline |
| Workers | Skills and service | Good jobs | Exit |

**The convening mechanism:** A Statewide Workforce Board with DJSIR, DE, DH, VPSC, universities, TAFEs, employers, unions, councils and Commonwealth.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public workforce dashboard of vacancies, pipeline, completion, retention, regional gaps and agency reliance.

**The leading indicator:** Training completions in priority occupations.
**The kill switch:** If retention falls or agency reliance rises, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true vacancy rate by occupation and region.
- The cost of turnover and agency reliance.
- The retention effect of pay, rostering and housing.
- The migration and settlement capacity.
- The public sector capability gap.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A workforce census.
- A retention trial.
- A regional workforce deal.
- A public sector capability assessment.
