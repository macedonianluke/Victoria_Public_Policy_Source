# Cost of Living and Household Budgets

> **Citizen promise:** Your household budget goes further, and the government knows what it actually costs you.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria delivers cost-of-living relief through dozens of rebates, concessions, fees and programs, but it does not add them up for the household. The next move is a **citizen budget**: measure what a household pays and receives across taxes, rates, fares, fees, fines, energy, housing and services; auto-enrol eligible households; and target relief to the people who need it.
>
> **2026 baseline:** The 2026–27 Budget describes more than **$2.5 billion** in cost-of-living support, including free public transport in April–May 2026, half-price fares until 1 January 2027, a 20% vehicle registration rebate, energy and rates concessions, and free daytime electricity for eligible households from October 2026. [A]

**Minister(s):** Treasurer (Colin Brooks); Minister for Finance and Government Services (Enver Erdogan); portfolio ministers for energy, transport, housing, health and families.
**Department(s):** Department of Treasury and Finance (DTF); Department of Government Services (DGS); portfolio departments.
**Key agencies:** State Revenue Office; Service Victoria; energy retailers; transport operators; councils; Centrelink (Commonwealth); consumer and community organisations.
**Statute / framework:** Taxation Acts; Essential Services Commission pricing; concession frameworks; consumer law; National Energy Retail Law.
**Scale:** More than **$2.5 billion** in cost-of-living support in 2026–27, plus tax, fee and concession settings. [A]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Households can afford the essentials.
- **Outcome 2:** Relief reaches the people who need it, automatically.
- **Outcome 3:** Prices and fees are transparent and competitive.
- **Outcome 4:** The tax and transfer system is fair.
- **Outcome 5:** The citizen can see what government costs and delivers.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Lower housing, energy and transport stress; higher concession take-up; fewer households in energy or rates debt; and a public household budget dashboard.

**What failure looks like:** More rebates, more complexity, and the same households falling behind.

**What the citizen experiences:** They hear about relief but cannot find it, qualify for one payment but not another, and face a rates, energy, water, transport and housing bill that is hard to compare or contest.
**What the frontline experiences:** Complex eligibility, manual processes, duplicated data and a fear of getting it wrong.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Universal subsidies:** Relief goes to households that do not need it, reducing the pool for those who do.
- **Low take-up:** Eligible households miss concessions because of complexity and stigma.
- **Multiple schemes:** Separate rebates, concessions and payments create duplication.
- **Price distortions:** Stamp duty, fees and charges can discourage mobility and work.
- **Fines and debt:** Penalties can trap people in poverty.
- **Energy and housing stress:** Bills and rents rise faster than incomes.
- **Data gaps:** The state does not know the total household cost.
- **Market concentration:** Limited competition in some markets raises prices.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. The biggest items are low take-up, universal subsidies and debt. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Cost of living is a set of rebates and concessions announced in the budget.

**The new frame:** The state manages a **citizen budget**: what a household pays and receives across all levels of government, and how to make it fair, simple and automatic.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. More rebates** | Add payments and concessions | Visible | Complexity and leakage | Not sufficient |
| **B. Citizen budget** | Measure total cost, auto-enrol, target relief, reform fees | Fair and simple | Requires data and reform | Preferred base |
| **C. Market reform** | Increase competition and transparency | Lower prices | Market resistance | Strong complement |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Auto-enrol, don't apply.** If the state knows a household is eligible, it should provide the concession automatically, with consent and privacy safeguards.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Household budget dashboard:** the total cost of taxes, rates, fares, fees, fines, energy, water and housing by household type and region.
- **Concession auto-enrolment:** use existing data to reach eligible households.
- **Targeted relief:** phase out universal payments to higher-income households.
- **Fee and fine reform:** proportional fines, payment plans and debt relief.
- **Price transparency:** energy, transport, housing and food.
- **Market competition:** consumer switching, benchmarks and enforcement.
- **Wage and income policy:** advocate Commonwealth tax and income support reform.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase out universal subsidies to higher-income households | DTF/portfolios | Targeted relief | Fiscal | [D] |
| Consolidate overlapping rebates and concessions | DTF/portfolios | One application/auto-enrol | Admin cost | [D] |
| Reform fines that trap people in poverty | DJCS/DTF | Proportional fines and payment plans | Debt and justice cost | [D] |
| Sunset low-value relief programs | DTF | Reallocate to targeted relief | Program savings | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate concession administration | DGS/agencies | Shared platform | Admin cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Means-testing and fee reform will be politically contested. The state must show who gains and who loses.
**What we will not cut:** Support for low-income households; essential concessions; crisis assistance.

**The leading indicator:** Concession take-up.
**The kill switch:** If relief is captured by higher-income households or increases debt, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The total household cost of government taxes, fees and charges.
- The concession take-up rate by cohort.
- The distributional impact of relief.
- The effect of fee and fine reform.
- The level of energy, housing and transport stress.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A household budget model.
- A concession auto-enrolment pilot.
- A fee and fine review.
- A price transparency trial.
