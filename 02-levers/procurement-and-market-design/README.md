# Procurement and Market Design

> **Citizen promise:** Your money buys public value, not just goods and services.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victorian government procurement is a compliance function when it should be a **strategy function**. The state spends tens of billions a year; it can use that demand to build local capability, create jobs, reduce emissions, improve service quality and grow social enterprise — if it buys outcomes, aggregates demand and manages contracts properly.
>
> **2026 baseline:** The Social Procurement Framework applies to goods, services and construction. Local Jobs First thresholds are **$1 million for regional projects** and **$3 million for metropolitan or statewide projects**; final Local Jobs First reforms commenced **1 July 2026**. [A]

**Minister(s):** Minister for Finance and Government Services (Enver Erdogan); portfolio ministers for major projects.
**Department(s):** Department of Government Services (DGS); Department of Treasury and Finance (DTF).
**Key agencies:** Victorian Government Purchasing Board (VGPB); Local Jobs First Commissioner; Industry Capability Network; Social Procurement Framework; HealthShare Victoria; Transport Victoria; councils.
**Scale:** Whole-of-government goods, services, construction and ICT spend; exact consolidated figure to be verified. [D]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** The state buys the outcome it actually wants, at the best whole-of-life cost.
- **Outcome 2:** Procurement builds Victorian capability, jobs and local supply chains.
- **Outcome 3:** Social and environmental outcomes are delivered through contracts, not just aspirational policies.
- **Outcome 4:** Contracts are managed, measured and exited well.
- **Outcome 5:** The market is competitive, transparent and fair.

**What success looks like by 2030:** A higher share of contracts paid on outcomes; more local content and social enterprise; fewer single-source and variation-heavy contracts; better supplier diversity; and measurable savings.

**What failure looks like:** More policy statements, more reporting, and the same consultant-heavy, variation-prone, low-competition contracts.

| Market | Suppliers, SMEs, social enterprises | Delivery | Market |

**What the citizen experiences:** They do not see procurement, but they experience its results: the hospital that opens late, the IT system that does not work, the local jobs that do not eventuate, the social enterprise that cannot get a contract.
**What the frontline experiences:** Complex rules, slow processes, limited market knowledge and weak contract management.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Fragmented demand:** Agencies buy the same things separately, losing scale and standards.
- **Activity contracts:** Contracts pay for hours and inputs, not outcomes.
- **Weak contract management:** Poor performance, variations and lock-in.
- **Consultant dependence:** The state buys advice instead of building capability.
- **Thin markets:** Regional and social enterprise suppliers are excluded by scale and process.
- **Local content leakage:** Major projects import skills and materials.
- **Reporting burden:** Suppliers face multiple systems and duplicated reporting.
- **Data gaps:** No whole-of-government view of spend, suppliers, outcomes or risk.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. Even a small improvement in contract performance and aggregation can be worth hundreds of millions. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Procurement is a transaction: specify, tender, award, pay.

**The new frame:** Procurement is **market design**: the state shapes what suppliers invest in, what they hire, what they build and how they innovate.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Compliance plus** | Better rules and reporting | Familiar | Does not change behaviour | Not sufficient |
| **B. Strategic procurement** | Category management, outcome contracts, supplier development, local content | Builds capability and value | Requires skill and discipline | Preferred base |
| **C. Public option / insourcing** | State delivers where markets fail | Control and resilience | Capability and cost risk | Use selectively |

**The reframe that matters most:** **Buy outcomes, not hours.** A contract should state the result, the measure, the price and the consequence — and the state should have the capability to manage it.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Category management:** one strategy per category, with spend, suppliers, risk and outcomes.
- **Outcome contracts:** pay for results, with clear metrics and exit rights.
- **Supplier development:** help SMEs, social enterprises and Aboriginal businesses bid and deliver.
- **Local content and skills plans:** enforced, not aspirational.
- **Contract management academy:** train public servants to manage performance.
- **Transparency:** publish contracts, variations, performance and outcomes.
- **Reverse tenders:** state the outcome and price; let the market propose.

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consolidate fragmented tenders into category strategies | DGS/agencies | Shared procurement | Scale and admin savings | [D] |
| Reduce consultant and labour-hire contracts | All agencies | Build in-house capability | Consultant spend | [A] |
| Sunset contracts that fail performance tests | Agencies | Re-tender or insource | Poor value | [D] |
| Reduce duplicate supplier reporting | DGS | One portal | Supplier time | [D] |
| Stop evaluating on price alone for outcome contracts | DGS/VGPB | Better value | Whole-of-life cost | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Insourcing and performance-based contracts will be contested by incumbents. The state must build the capability to manage the transition.
**What we will not cut:** Fair competition; probity; support for SMEs and social enterprises.

| Add / build / buy / guarantee | Why | Who delivers | Year 1 cost | Year 2+ cost | Offset |

| Public contract register | Transparency | DGS | Low | Operating | Existing data |

**The new capability:** The public service must be able to **design markets, write outcome contracts, manage performance and exit**.

**The new deal:** The citizen gets better services and local jobs. The supplier gets a fair, predictable market. The state gets value and capability.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Industry / suppliers | Quality, innovation, local content | Predictable pipeline | Exclusion |
| SMEs / social enterprises | Agility and social outcomes | Access and support | Thin markets |
| Unions / workers | Skills and fair work | Good jobs | Labour risk |
| Councils | Aggregated demand | Better prices | Duplication |
| Commonwealth | Aligned rules | Better national outcomes | Fragmentation |
| VAGO / IBAC | Oversight | Integrity | Risk |
| Citizens | Patience | Better services | Distrust |

**The convening mechanism:** A Procurement and Market Design Board with DGS, DTF, agencies, industry, social enterprise, unions and councils.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public procurement dashboard of spend, competition, local content, social outcomes, variations and performance.

## 10. Measures that matter

| Dimension | Metric | Baseline | Target | Source | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Value | Whole-of-life cost savings | To be baselined | Up | DGS | Annual |
| Outcomes | Share of contracts paid on outcomes | To be baselined | Up | DGS | Annual |
| Competition | Share of contracts with 3+ bidders | To be baselined | Up | DGS | Annual |
| Local | Victorian content and jobs | To be baselined | Up | Local Jobs First | Annual |
| Social | Social procurement outcomes | To be baselined | Up | DGS | Annual |
| Performance | Contract variations | To be baselined | Down | DGS | Annual |
| Capability | Contract managers trained | To be baselined | Up | VPSC | Annual |
| Integrity | Procurement complaints | To be baselined | Down | IBAC | Annual |

**The one number that matters most:** The share of spend that buys a measured outcome.
**The leading indicator:** The number of category strategies in place.
**The kill switch:** If procurement reform reduces competition or increases risk, stop and redesign.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The consolidated whole-of-government procurement spend.
- The share of contracts with outcome measures.
- The local content and social procurement results.
- The cost of procurement administration.
- The market capacity of social enterprises and SMEs.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A spend and supplier baseline.
- An outcome contract pilot.
- A supplier development trial.
- A contract management capability census.
