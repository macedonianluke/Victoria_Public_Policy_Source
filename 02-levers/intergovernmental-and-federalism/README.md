# Intergovernmental and Federalism

> **Citizen promise:** Victoria gets a fair deal from the federation, and spends it on outcomes.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Many of Victoria's biggest levers — hospitals, schools, housing, water, energy, NDIS, tax — are shared with the Commonwealth. Victoria often absorbs Commonwealth conditions and cost-shifting instead of negotiating outcomes. The next move is a **federation strategy**: one Victorian position, outcome-based agreements, pooled funding and a serious tax reform agenda.
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria's 2026–27 Pre-Election Budget Update forecasts **$173.1 billion** net debt and **$8.9 billion** interest expense, while the Commonwealth–state funding architecture shapes hospitals, schools, housing and infrastructure. [A]

**Minister(s):** Premier (Ben Carroll); Treasurer (Colin Brooks); Minister for Government Services and Finance (Enver Erdogan); portfolio ministers.
**Department(s):** Department of Premier and Cabinet (DPC); Department of Treasury and Finance (DTF).
**Key agencies:** Commonwealth Treasury; Department of Finance; National Cabinet; COAG/First Ministers' meetings; National Housing Supply and Affordability Council; Infrastructure Australia; Productivity Commission; state and territory counterparts.
**Statute / framework:** Intergovernmental agreements; National Health Reform Agreement; National School Reform Agreement; Housing Australia Future Fund; Murray–Darling Basin Plan; GST distribution; National Disability Insurance Scheme.
**Scale:** Commonwealth transfers and national agreements shape a large share of Victoria's budget. [A]

---

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Victoria gets fair and predictable federal funding.
- **Outcome 2:** National agreements are based on outcomes, not inputs and conditions.
- **Outcome 3:** Cost-shifting and duplication are reduced.
- **Outcome 4:** Tax reform is progressed nationally and in Victoria.
- **Outcome 5:** Victoria uses its federation relationships to solve cross-border problems.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Fair funding; fewer reporting conditions; pooled funding for shared outcomes; tax reform; and better national coordination on housing, health, energy and water.

**What failure looks like:** More agreements, more conditions, more cost-shifting, and Victoria blamed for national failures.

## 3. Where the money and time leak

- **Cost-shifting:** One government saves money by transferring cost to another.
- **Input controls:** Commonwealth agreements specify inputs rather than outcomes.
- **Duplicated reporting:** States report similar data to multiple Commonwealth agencies.
- **Fragmented programs:** Medicare, NDIS, aged care, housing and schools do not join up.
- **Tax inefficiency:** Stamp duty, payroll tax and inefficient national taxes distort behaviour.
- **Infrastructure gaps:** National and state project selection is not coordinated.
- **Water and energy:** National market and basin rules shape local outcomes.
- **Treaty and First Peoples:** Commonwealth and state responsibilities overlap.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** To be costed. Cost-shifting and duplicated reporting are significant but poorly measured. [D]

---

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Victoria implements Commonwealth agreements and advocates for more funding.

**The new frame:** Victoria negotiates **outcome-based federation deals** with pooled funding, shared data and clear accountability.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Advocacy** | Ask for more money and fewer conditions | Familiar | Limited leverage | Not sufficient |
| **B. Outcome agreements** | Pool funding, agree outcomes, share data | Better value | Requires trust | Preferred base |
| **C. Tax and federation reform** | Broader-based taxes, fair GST, clearer roles | Structural | National politics | Long-term direction |

**The reframe that matters most:** **One Victorian position, negotiated on outcomes.** The Premier and Treasurer should have a single, published federation strategy, not 20 portfolio positions.

**Supporting moves:**

- **Federation audit:** map every agreement, funding stream, condition and reporting requirement.
- **Outcome-based agreements:** negotiate pooled funding and shared metrics.
- **Tax reform:** replace stamp duty with land tax over time; reform payroll tax; advocate fair GST.
- **City and region deals:** bundle transport, housing, skills and energy.
- **Data sharing:** common national data and evaluation.
- **Treaty interface:** clarify Commonwealth and state roles.

**What we will not cut:** Essential funding; national standards; intergovernmental cooperation.

## 7. The ecosystem deal

| Party | What they give | What they get | What happens if they do not move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commonwealth | Fair funding and flexible agreements | Better national outcomes | State absorbs costs |
| Other states | Collective negotiation | Fairer federation | Competition |
| Local government | Delivery and data | Federal and state funding | Cost-shifting |
| Business / community | Evidence and advocacy | Better services | Fragmentation |
| First Peoples | Treaty and self-determination | Clear roles | Conflict |
| Productivity Commission / Infrastructure Australia | Independent advice | Better decisions | Poor investment |

**The convening mechanism:** A Federation Cabinet Committee chaired by the Premier, with the Treasurer and portfolio ministers.
**The accountability mechanism:** A public federation dashboard of funding, agreements, conditions, outcomes and cost-shifting.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The full value and conditions of every Commonwealth agreement.
- The cost-shifting value by portfolio.
- The distributional impact of tax reform.
- The best model for pooled funding.
- The Commonwealth's appetite for reform.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A federation audit.
- A pooled funding pilot.
- A tax reform analysis.
- A national data agreement.
