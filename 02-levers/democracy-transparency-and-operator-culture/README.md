# Democracy, Transparency and the Operator Culture

> **Citizen promise:** You can see who decided, why, who influenced it, what it cost — and you can change it.
>
> **Operator thesis:** Victoria does not have a participation problem. It has a **feedback problem**. Elections collect a signal every four years, then the system loses the citizen until the next one. The fix is not more consultation. It is a continuous, transparent, evidence-led democracy: **publish the decision record, disclose the influence, deliberate before the decision, legislate the plumbing, and make the operator — not the slogan — the unit of credibility.**
>
> **2026 baseline:** Victoria has donation caps, a lobbyist register, quarterly ministerial diaries, a parliamentary standards commission and a recent upper-house voting reform. It still has an FOI Act from 1982, no general truth-in-political-advertising law, incomplete lobbying disclosure, and a culture that rewards the announcement more than the implementation. [A]

**Minister(s):** Premier (Ben Carroll); Special Minister of State (Ingrid Stitt); Attorney-General (Sonya Kilkenny); Minister for Government Services and Finance (Enver Erdogan).
**Department(s):** Department of Premier and Cabinet (DPC); Department of Justice and Community Safety (DJCS); Department of Government Services (DGS).
**Key agencies:** Parliament of Victoria; Victorian Electoral Commission (VEC); Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission (IBAC); Victorian Auditor-General's Office (VAGO); Victorian Ombudsman; Office of the Victorian Information Commissioner (OVIC); Parliamentary Workplace Standards and Integrity Commission (PWSIC); Parliamentary Budget Office (PBO); courts; 79 local councils; Chief Municipal Inspector.
**Statute / framework:** Constitution Act 1975; Electoral Act 2002; Freedom of Information Act 1982; Members of Parliament (Standards) Act 1978; Public Administration Act 2004; Local Government Act 2020; Audit Act 1994; IBAC Act 2011; Ombudsman Act 1973; Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014.
**Scale:** Whole-of-state democratic system: 128 state MPs, 79 councils, a large public service, and an ecosystem of media, civil society, unions, business and citizens. [B]

## 1. The outcome

- **Outcome 1:** Citizens can see the evidence, the decision, the influence and the cost.
- **Outcome 2:** Influence is disclosed in real time, not reconstructed after a scandal.
- **Outcome 3:** Major decisions are deliberated with the people they affect, before they are locked in.
- **Outcome 4:** Parliament scrutinises, legislates and reviews with real resources and real consequences.
- **Outcome 5:** Government operates as an operator: baseline, cost, offset, owner, metric, review.
- **Outcome 6:** Trust is earned through delivery, not asserted through advertising.

**What success looks like by 2030:** Higher trust; faster and more proactive information release; real-time lobbying and donation disclosure; truth-in-advertising enforced; a standing citizens' panel with a government response requirement; stronger parliamentary committees; and every major decision accompanied by a public decision record.

**What failure looks like:** More consultation, more strategies, more advertising, and the same sense that decisions are made before the public is told.

**What the citizen experiences:** An election every four years, a flood of announcements, a consultation portal, an FOI process that takes months, and a decision that often feels pre-determined. They can vote, but they cannot easily see who influenced the decision or what it will cost.
**What the frontline experiences:** Risk aversion, blame, short-termism, duplicated consultation, and a communications machine that treats policy as a campaign.

## 3. Where trust, money and time leak

- **The slogan cycle:** Announcements without a baseline, cost, offset, owner, metric or review date. The public cannot tell what changed, what it cost or whether it worked.
- **Influence opacity:** The lobbyist register and ministerial diaries are useful but incomplete. Cabinet, internal meetings, in-house advocates, consultants and former ministers are not fully covered. [A]
- **Money and campaigns:** Donation caps and disclosure exist, but disclosure is periodic, expenditure and third-party campaigning are less visible, and there is no general truth-in-advertising law. [A]
- **Information access:** The FOI Act 1982 is slow, formal and exemption-heavy. Parliament's committee has recommended a "push" model; reform is still in development. [A]
- **Parliamentary scrutiny:** Question time, committee resourcing, late bills, delegated legislation and post-legislative review are not consistently designed for scrutiny.
- **Local democracy:** Rate caps, engagement quality, councillor conduct and transparency vary widely across 79 councils. The 2026 reforms improve some settings but do not create a consistent transparency standard. [A]
- **Public appointments:** Boards and agencies are not consistently merit-based, skills-based or publicly explained.
- **Procurement and grants:** Contracts, variations, beneficial owners and outcomes are hard to scrutinise.
- **AI and algorithms:** Public-sector AI use is growing faster than the register, impact assessment and redress.
- **Civic capability:** Civics education, media literacy and public-service operator skills are under-invested.
- **Culture:** The system rewards the launch, the media release and the ribbon-cutting; it does not reward the boring, measurable, ten-year delivery.

**Estimated annual value of the leak:** Hard to quantify, but the cost shows up as poor decisions, repeated consultations, failed projects, lost trust and lower compliance. [D]

## 4. The reframe

**The old frame:** Democracy is an election every four years. Transparency is an FOI request. Participation is a consultation. Accountability is a question in Parliament.

**The new frame:** Democracy is a **continuous feedback loop**. Transparency is **open by default**. Participation is **deliberation before the decision**. Accountability is **a public decision record with an owner, a metric and a review date**.

**Three alternative models:**

| Model | How it works | Upside | Downside | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Election-plus** | Keep the current system; add a few transparency measures | Familiar; low risk | Does not change the culture or the feedback loop | Not sufficient |
| **B. Open, deliberative, accountable democracy** | Decision records, real-time influence disclosure, push FOI, citizens' panel, parliamentary reform | Trust, quality, legitimacy | Requires political courage and resources | Preferred base |
| **C. Co-governed democracy** | Participatory budgeting, treaty-based shared decision-making, local co-design, sortition | Deep legitimacy and ownership | Time, representativeness, governance complexity | Use for selected decisions |

**The reframe that matters most:** **No decision without a public decision record.** Every significant public decision gets a one-page record:

- the problem and the evidence
- the options considered
- who was consulted and what they said
- who influenced the decision
- the cost and the offset
- the owner and the delivery plan
- the metric and the review date
- the kill switch

This is the cultural and operational core. It changes what it means to be a competent minister or public servant: not "I announced it", but "I can show the record, the delivery and the result".

**Supporting moves:**

- **Show the math.** Every announcement includes baseline, cost, offset, owner, metric and date.
- **Open the books.** Contracts, grants, appointments, beneficial owners and outcomes in one place.
- **Disclose the influence.** Real-time lobbying and ministerial meeting registers, with clear definitions and sanctions.
- **Deliberate before you decide.** A standing citizens' panel, participatory budgeting and public deliberation for major long-term choices.
- **Legislate the plumbing.** FOI, donations, truth in advertising, parliamentary standards, public appointments, local government transparency.
- **Fund the scrutiny.** Committees, audit, Ombudsman, IBAC and OVIC need resources and follow-up.
- **Protect the whistleblower.** Independent support, anti-retaliation and a duty to act.
- **Teach the citizen.** Civics, media literacy and adult civic education.
- **Report to citizens annually.** What was promised, what was delivered, what was stopped, what it cost.

### The Anti-Salesman standard

The cultural problem is not just the rules. It is the **salesman politician** model: scripted, focus-grouped, risk-averse, and incentivised by the next job rather than the current one. The alternative is the **civil-servant representative**: unscripted, numerate, transparent and temporary.

| Old "salesman politician" | New "civil-servant representative" |
|---|---|
| 45-second doorstop; three-word slogans; curated reels | 2–3 hour uncut podcasts; live whiteboard town halls; raw spreadsheets |
| "Block and bridge" back to rehearsed lines | Answer the exact premise; steel-man the trade-off; cite primary data |
| Never admit uncertainty; blame predecessors or Canberra | Confidence intervals; pre-committed 90-day fix/kill metrics |
| 80% spin doctors and media monitors | 80% forensic data analysts, legal drafters and casework auditors |

**The Civil-Servant Deed.** A voluntary commitment that candidates can sign before the election, and that can become law where required:

- **Three-hour uncut standard:** one unedited long-form session or open town hall every month, with no pre-submitted questions and no edit rights.
- **Five-part anti-spin answer:** the problem in numbers; the strongest argument against; the trade-off; the metric that proves it in 12 months; what we will change if it fails.
- **Show your working:** every policy release attaches the raw spreadsheet, assumptions and independent costing.
- **24-hour diary API:** every external meeting, attendee, client and subject, published within 24 hours.
- **15-day Cabinet release:** Cabinet papers and minutes published within 15 business days, with OVIC certifying any redaction and a declassification date.
- **Open expenditure ledger:** contracts, consultancies, grants, variations and MP expenses above $1,000, published within 48 hours with supplier, tender method and approving delegate.
- **Casework bug tracker:** every constituent case gets a timestamped ticket and an agency response time.
- **Voting justification log:** a 150-word plain-English explanation of every vote, in the member's own words, within 48 hours.
- **Monthly frontline shift:** one unannounced, camera-free shift with a nurse, teacher, police officer, tradie or small business owner.
- **Seven-year revolving-door ban:** no lobbying, government-affairs, Big Four advisory partnership or contractor board role for seven years after leaving office.
- **Term limits, blind trusts and at-risk pay:** a maximum of two to three terms; investments in a blind trust; 20% of ministerial salary held in escrow against audited portfolio KPIs.

**The test is not "can you sell it?" but "can you defend it for three hours, unscripted, with the spreadsheet open?"**

## 5. Cut here

| Cut / stop / move | Current owner | What happens to the people | Saving or capacity released | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Announcements without baseline, cost, offset, owner, metric or review | All ministers and departments | Policy becomes a public decision record | Less rework, better delivery | [D] |
| Late bills without exposure or scrutiny (except genuine emergencies) | Parliament/executive | 30-day exposure and committee review | Better law, fewer amendments | [D] |
| Duplicated consultation exercises | Departments/councils | One public engagement record per decision | Admin time and public goodwill | [D] |
| Ministerial diary blackouts for external meetings | DPC/ministers | Monthly external meeting disclosure | Trust | [D] |
| Lobbying loopholes: in-house advocates, former ministers, consultants | DPC/Parliament | Broader register and cooling-off | Integrity | [D] |
| Scripted slogans, block-and-bridge and glossy brochures | Ministers/agencies | Three-hour uncut sessions and raw spreadsheets | Trust and quality | [D] |
| Cabinet secrecy beyond genuine confidentiality | DPC/Cabinet | 15-day release with OVIC-certified redactions | Better decisions and trust | [D] |
| Encrypted auto-delete channels for public business | Ministers/advisers | Preserved records and IBAC referral | Integrity | [D] |
| Secret crossbench side letters and midnight deals | Parliament/executive | 72-hour public deal ledger and PBO costing | Better law | [D] |
| Ministerial override of grant merit scores | Ministers/agencies | Published scores and PAEC appearance | Fairness | [D] |
| Lobbyist revolving door and post-office advisory roles | DPC/IBAC | Seven-year ban and salary disgorgement | Integrity | [D] |
| FOI fees for personal information and excessive exemptions | OVIC/agencies | Push model, 30-day clock, narrow exemptions | Access and trust | [D] |
| Uncompetitive public appointments | DPC/boards | Merit panels and published registers | Quality and trust | [D] |
| Non-essential government advertising and PR | DPC/agencies | Public information only | Spend and trust | [D] |
| Deferred local government transparency | Local Government Victoria/councils | A consistent local transparency charter | Local trust | [D] |

**The hard calls:** Real-time influence disclosure, truth-in-advertising and FOI reform will be resisted by incumbents, lobbyists and parts of the media. They are the price of a functioning democracy.
**What we will not cut:** Legitimate access to ministers; confidential Cabinet deliberation where it is genuinely necessary; privacy; national security; the ability of citizens to seek help.

**The convening mechanism:** A Democracy and Integrity Cabinet Committee, with Parliament, the integrity bodies, local government, civil society and citizens.
**The accountability mechanism:** An annual State of Democracy report, a public trust dashboard, and a parliamentary joint committee on democratic reform.

## 11. Open questions and sources

**What we do not know:**

- The true cost of FOI administration and delay.
- The extent of undisclosed lobbying and in-house advocacy.
- The effect of real-time disclosure on donations and participation.
- The best model for a standing citizens' panel.
- The impact of truth-in-advertising on misinformation and political competition.
- The effect of parliamentary reform on scrutiny and public trust.
- The cost and representativeness of participatory budgeting.

**What we will commission or test in the first year:**

- A transparency audit across FOI, lobbying, contracts, grants and appointments.
- A citizens' panel pilot on a major long-term decision.
- A truth-in-advertising and digital ad register trial.
- A participatory budgeting pilot in three councils.
- An evaluation of public decision records.
