# The Democratic Reform List

**Thirty-plus changes to improve the democratic system itself — not just the policies it produces.**

This is a list of rules-of-the-game changes: information, money, influence, Parliament, elections, local democracy and the culture of government. Some need legislation. Some need budget decisions. Some need only a minister willing to publish the record.

The list is ordered by the strength of the case, not by political difficulty.

---

## The principles

1. **No decision without a public decision record.**
2. **No new power without a check.**
3. **No transparency without privacy and security.**
4. **No participation without a decision.**
5. **No legislation without review.**
6. **No reform without a cost, an owner and a date.**

---

## A. Information and open government

| # | Change | Why it matters | Vehicle | Timing | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Replace the FOI Act 1982 with a Right to Information Act** — push model, 30-day clock, no fees for personal information, narrow exemptions, public interest test, independent review | The current system is slow, formal and exemption-heavy; Parliament's committee has recommended a modern push model | New Act; OVIC reform | Year 1 | Agency capacity, privacy, security |
| 2 | **Mandatory proactive release schedules** for departments, agencies and councils, with an annual compliance report | Most information should be published before it is requested | Ministerial directions + FOI Act | Year 1 | Records management |
| 3 | **Public Decision Record** for every significant Cabinet, planning, procurement and grants decision, published within 30 days | Shows the evidence, options, influence, cost, owner, metric and review date | Cabinet handbook + legislation for statutory decisions | Day 1 policy; Year 1 law | Bureaucratic resistance |
| 4 | **Open contracts, grants and appointments register** — searchable, machine-readable, with beneficiary and outcome | Citizens should be able to follow public money | DGS/DTF platform | Year 1 | Commercial sensitivity |
| 5 | **Public beneficial ownership register** for government contractors, lobbyists and their clients | Stops hidden ownership and influence | New Act / procurement rules | Year 2 | Privacy, enforcement |
| 6 | **AI and algorithm register + impact assessments** for public-sector automated decisions, with a right to human review and redress | AI is entering decisions faster than accountability | DGS/OVIC framework + legislation | Year 1 pilot; Year 2 law | Vendor resistance, capability |

## C. Parliament and accountability

| # | Change | Why it matters | Vehicle | Timing | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Independent Parliamentary Standards Commissioner**, or a strengthened PWSIC, with investigation and sanction powers covering Members and Ministers | The current standards system is split and does not cover the Ministerial Code | Members of Parliament (Standards) Act; new Act | Year 1 | Parliament's control of its own affairs |
| 14 | **Question time reform** — fixed number of questions, no Dorothy Dixers, longer answers, fact-check support | Question time is often a government broadcast, not scrutiny | Standing Orders | Year 1 | Government majority |
| 15 | **Committee reform** — guaranteed resources, minority reports, government response deadlines, public evidence | Committees are the best scrutiny tool the Parliament has | Standing Orders + budget | Year 1 | Executive control |
| 16 | **Legislative scrutiny** — 30-day exposure draft for major bills, regulatory impact statement, human rights and treaty compatibility statements, post-legislative review after three years | Too much law is made late, without evidence or review | Standing Orders + Legislation Act | Year 1 | Urgency, drafting capacity |
| 17 | **Delegated legislation reform** — sunset, disallowance, public register, scrutiny committee | Much of the regulatory state is made by regulation, not legislation | Legislation Act | Year 1 | Executive convenience |
| 18 | **Expand the Parliamentary Budget Office** — cost all election commitments and major bills, publish assumptions | Independent costings are a public good | Parliamentary Budget Office Act | Year 1 | Fiscal and political resistance |

## G. The Anti-Salesman standard

These are the cultural and incentive changes that make the rest credible. Some can start tomorrow without legislation. Others need it.

| # | Change | Why it matters | Vehicle | Timing | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 31 | **15-day Cabinet release** — Cabinet papers and minutes published within 15 business days, with OVIC-certified redactions and a declassification date | Cabinet secrecy is used to hide advice, not just protect deliberation | Cabinet handbook + FOI Act | Year 1 | Cabinet resistance |
| 32 | **24-hour diary API + encrypted auto-delete ban + 72-hour crossbench deal ledger** | Real-time influence disclosure and an end to midnight side letters | Ministerial Code + Standing Orders | Day 1 direction; Year 1 law | Enforcement, privacy |
| 33 | **Open expenditure ledger** — contracts, consultancies, grants, variations and MP expenses above $1,000, published within 48 hours; milestone escrow for grants; PAEC appearance for ministerial overrides | Taxpayers should see every dollar, not wait for an audit | Financial Management Act + procurement rules | Year 1 pilot; Year 2 law | Data quality, commercial sensitivity |
| 34 | **Constituent casework ledger + quarterly friction report + voting justification log + monthly frontline shifts** | Re-engineer the MP's job around citizen service and systemic bottlenecks | Parliamentary rules + Members' standards | Year 1 | MP resistance, privacy |
| 35 | **Seven-year revolving-door ban + term limits + blind trusts + 20% at-risk ministerial pay** | Change the incentives from the next job to the current job | New Act / Constitution Act / Members' standards; legal review required | Year 2 | Constitutional and industrial risk |
| 36 | **Anti-Salesman communication standard** — monthly three-hour uncut sessions, five-part anti-spin answers, raw spreadsheets and assumptions with every policy | Replaces the slogan with the working | Voluntary deed + ministerial code + budget circular | Day 1 | Culture, format |
| 37 | **Civic Draft, forensic vetting, citizens' preselection jury and Civil-Servant Deed** | Recruit operators, not careerists; let candidates model the reform before government | Party rules / civil society movement / public funding conditions | Year 1 | Party resistance, representativeness |

**The point of the Anti-Salesman standard:** the cultural shift is not a communications strategy. It is a change in what counts as competence. The operator shows the ledger, names the trade-off, accepts the metric and sits for the unscripted examination. The salesman recites the slogan and hides the contract.

## Sequencing

### First 100 days — executive and administrative

- Publish the Public Decision Record template.
- Publish a transparency audit: FOI, diaries, lobbying, contracts, grants, appointments.
- Announce the FOI reform bill and 30-day standard.
- Launch real-time lobbying and ministerial meeting registers.
- Announce real-time donations and truth-in-advertising reform.
- Establish the standing citizens' panel and announce its first question.
- Pilot decision records in three departments.
- Launch participatory budgeting pilots in three councils.
- Publish the parliamentary reform white paper.

### Year 1 — legislation and budget

- Right to Information Act.
- Electoral Amendment (real-time disclosure, expenditure caps, truth in advertising, digital ad register).
- Lobbying reform.
- Parliamentary Standards and Integrity reform.
- Standing Orders reform.
- Public appointments register and merit panels.
- Local government transparency charter.
- AI/algorithm register.
- Operator standard and annual citizen report card.
- Citizens' panel pilot evaluation.
- 15-day Cabinet release and 24-hour diary API.
- Open expenditure ledger pilot.
- Casework ledger and voting justification pilot.
- Civil-Servant Deed and three-hour uncut standard.

### Year 2–4 — structural reform

- Citizens' panel statutory basis.
- Participatory budgeting expansion.
- Upper house structure review.
- Post-legislative review.
- Beneficial ownership register.
- Open contracts and grants API.
- Local government integrity reform.
- Seven-year revolving-door ban.
- Term limits, blind trusts and at-risk ministerial pay (with legal review).
- Civic Draft and citizens' preselection jury.
- Evaluation of trust, participation and delivery outcomes.

## The big risks and how to handle them

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **FOI reform overwhelms agencies** | A push model needs records, systems and culture | Phase it; fund records management; publish schedules; OVIC oversight |
| **Real-time disclosure creates privacy or security risks** | Donors, lobbyists and citizens have legitimate privacy | Thresholds, redaction, security, independent oversight |
| **Truth-in-advertising chills political speech** | The law must target clearly false statements, not opinion | Narrow definition, rapid correction, appeal, statutory review |
| **Lobbying reform is gamed** | In-house advocates, consultants and former ministers can evade | Broad definition, cooling-off, client disclosure, sanctions |
| **Citizens' panel is captured or unrepresentative** | Legitimacy depends on selection and facilitation | Random selection, demographic balance, independent facilitation, published methods |
| **Parliamentary reform is blocked by the executive** | The government controls the House | Public package, committee support, crossbench negotiation, budget |
| **Local transparency is unfunded** | Councils have different capacity | Shared platforms, funding, minimum standards |
| **Transparency becomes surveillance** | Publishing personal data can harm citizens | Privacy impact assessment, data minimisation, consent |
| **Cabinet release chills frank advice** | Ministers and public servants need space to deliberate | 15-day delay, narrow redactions, OVIC certification, declassification dates |
| **Open ledger creates commercial and privacy risk** | Contract and grant data can expose individuals or trade secrets | Thresholds, redaction, beneficial ownership, independent audit |
| **Term limits weaken institutional memory** | Experience matters, and limits can empower permanent staff | Pair with a strong public service, transition support and a review clause |
| **Casework data exposes vulnerable people** | Constituent cases often involve personal information | Privacy-protected tickets, aggregate reporting, consent |
| **Three-hour standard becomes theatre** | A long session can still be a performance | Publish the data, the trade-off and the kill metric; independent moderation |

## What not to do

- Do not create a new integrity body for every problem. Use the ones you have, resource them and enforce their findings.
- Do not confuse consultation with decision. Publish what changed and why.
- Do not publish private data in the name of transparency.
- Do not legislate in haste without a review clause.
- Do not let the perfect be the enemy of the pilot.
- Do not announce a reform without the baseline, the cost and the owner.

---

## The test

A democratic reform is real when a citizen can answer six questions without an FOI request:

1. **Who decided?**
2. **On what evidence?**
3. **Who influenced them?**
4. **What did it cost, and who paid?**
5. **When will it be reviewed, and how do I challenge it?**
6. **Can the decision-maker defend it for three hours, unscripted, with the spreadsheet open?**

If the answer is "you can't", the reform is not finished.
