# Cutting Overhead Without Cutting Care

**A proposal to consolidate Victoria's non-frontline bureaucracy, shared services and shadow workforce — and reinvest the dividend in frontline capability.**

## 1. The citizen promise

**Your taxes buy care, not corporate overhead.** The state can remove a substantial layer of duplicated administration, external consultants, unused software, surplus leases and promotional spending without cutting a single nurse, teacher, paramedic, firefighter or police officer.

The review's headline finding is a **$6.2 billion to $8.6 billion annual non-frontline savings potential**, realised over 24–36 months. That is a modelled range, not an audited number. The purpose of this proposal is to test it, not to bank it.

---

## 2. Why now

Victoria's fiscal position is tight: net debt is forecast at **$173.1 billion** at 30 June 2027, with **$8.9 billion** of interest expense. [A] The state cannot tax or cut its way to a sustainable budget without improving the productivity of the non-frontline system.

At the same time, the government has already accepted the direction: the 2025 Silver Review found the public service had become top-heavy, and the 2026 response targets executive roles, consultant spend, shared services and entity consolidation. [A] The question is whether the reform goes deep enough and whether the savings are real.

The review's component estimates:

- **$2.2b–$2.8b/year** from returning non-frontline staffing ratios to pre-pandemic benchmarks and consolidating statutory body back offices.
- **$1.2b–$1.6b/year** from merging 76 hospital corporate back offices into regional shared-service hubs, while protecting every bed and clinician.
- **$0.6b–$0.9b/year** from trimming central education head-office bureaucracy by 30% and unifying TAFE back-office functions.
- **$1.5b–$2.2b/year** from a 40%–50% reduction in departmental labour hire, advisory firms and infrastructure delivery authority subcontracting.
- **$350m–$550m/year** from software licence consolidation, SaaS shelfware removal and halting bespoke agency IT builds.
- **$200m–$300m/year** from CBD lease consolidation and passenger fleet reduction.
- **$100m–$150m/year** from banning non-essential promotional advertising and halving agency PR units.

## 4. The operating model

**Old model:** Every entity has its own board, CEO, HR, finance, legal, IT, procurement, communications and strategy function. The centre coordinates; nobody consolidates.

**New model:** **One back office, many front doors.** Common corporate platforms and shared-service hubs serve portfolios, while frontline teams retain operational control.

### The moves

1. **Shared-services utility.** Consolidate HR, payroll, finance, procurement, legal, IT and cyber into a small number of regional/whole-of-government hubs, with service-level agreements and public benchmarks.
2. **Health back-office consolidation.** Merge the corporate functions of the 76 health services into regional hubs. Protect beds, wards, clinicians and patient-facing roles. Keep local clinical governance.
3. **Education and TAFE shared services.** One back office for payroll, HR, procurement, marketing and IT across TAFEs; reduce central compliance reporting to principals.
4. **Shadow workforce cap.** A hard 40%–50% reduction in departmental labour hire, advisory firms and sub-consultants, with a public register of contracts, rates and outcomes.
5. **Software and lease reset.** Single enterprise agreements for productivity, cloud, cyber, HR and finance software; exit surplus CBD leases; shrink the executive vehicle pool.
6. **Advertising and PR discipline.** Ban non-essential promotional advertising; retain public safety, health and emergency campaigns; halve agency PR units.
7. **Redeploy, retrain, protect the frontline.** Use natural attrition, fixed-term non-renewal and voluntary transition first. Offer retraining into frontline and digital roles. No forced frontline redundancies.
8. **Reinvest the dividend.** Split the verified net saving between debt reduction, tax relief and frontline capability, with a public formula.

## 5. Cut / add ledger

| Cut | Add | Why it is better |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicated corporate functions across departments, hospitals and TAFEs | Shared-service hubs and common platforms | Same output, lower cost, clearer accountability |
| External consultants and labour hire | Permanent public capability and a contract register | Less dependence, better value |
| Unused software licences and bespoke IT | Enterprise licensing and reusable platforms | Lower run cost, better security |
| Surplus CBD leases and executive vehicles | Hybrid work and a smaller fleet | Lower occupancy cost |
| Non-essential advertising and PR | Public information and frontline communication | Less spin, more trust |
| Executive layers and duplicated boards | Flatter spans, fewer entities | Faster decisions, clearer responsibility |
| Deferred frontline capability | Retraining, digital tools and service improvement | The dividend is visible to the citizen |

## 6. The hard risks

| Risk | Why it matters | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Savings are modelled, not audited** | The $6.2b–$8.6b range could be overstated | Publish the baseline; use a phased, verified savings gate; independent audit |
| **Frontline work bounces back** | Admin cuts can dump paperwork on nurses, teachers and police | Frontline firewall; admin-hour measurement; stop-work authority |
| **Shared services fail** | Poorly designed shared services can be slower and more expensive | Pilot, benchmark, service-level agreements, exit rights |
| **Redundancy costs hit Year 1** | Forced separations can consume the savings | Natural attrition, fixed-term non-renewal, voluntary packages, multi-yearphasing |
| **Industrial action** | Unions will resist headcount cuts | Genuine consultation, retraining, no frontline cuts, transparent numbers |
| **Commonwealth and GST effects** | Savings can affect funding relativities | Model the federal fiscal effects before announcement |
| **Capability loss** | Cutting too deep destroys the ability to manage contracts and reform | Protect and rebuild contract management, digital and financial capability |

**The one number that matters most:** Verified net recurring savings, after transition costs and redundancy.
**The kill switch:** If frontline admin hours rise, or service outcomes fall, stop and redesign.
