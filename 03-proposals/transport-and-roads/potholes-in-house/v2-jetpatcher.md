# Policy Proposal: Bringing Victorian Pothole & Road Patching Back In-House

**Executive Summary:** This policy establishes a state-owned, publicly operated rapid-response patching fleet of **50 single-operator automated patching trucks (Jetpatchers)** capable of repairing up to **500,000 potholes annually**. By using bulk state procurement leverage (~18% discount) and reallocating **358 duplicated back-office and executive roles** across the transport bureaucracy—with voluntary pathways for displaced staff to retrain into frontline road operations—the program is **100% budget-neutral in Year 1** and delivers **$28.75 million in permanent structural savings** from Year 2 onward.

---

## 1. The Problem: Why the Current Model Fails
* **Severe Asset Decay:** According to the Victorian Auditor-General's Office (VAGO) audit *Maintaining State Roads* (September 2026), **48% of Victoria’s state road network is in poor or very poor condition**, accompanied by a **$1.34 billion** maintenance backlog.
* **Fragmented Ownership & Contractor Mark-Ups:** The Victorian State Government currently owns zero automated patching trucks, relying instead on multi-layered private maintenance contracts (e.g., Downer, Fulton Hogan, Ventia) that add corporate profit margins, slow dispatch times, and weaken in-house engineering oversight. Meanwhile, individual rural councils (such as Hepburn Shire and Moyne Shire) purchase single trucks at full retail prices.
* **Top-Heavy Transport Bureaucracy:** The Department of Transport and Planning (DTP) and its infrastructure delivery arm, the Victorian Infrastructure Delivery Authority (VIDA), employ **more than 7,000 staff** across overlapping HR, communications, policy, legal, and executive divisions.

---

## 2. Operational Blueprint: The 50-Truck Public Fleet
* **Annual Repair Target:** **500,000 potholes patched per year**, matching the statewide benchmark set under the Transport Victoria 2026–27 Road Maintenance Program.
* **Fleet Capacity & Output Maths:**
  * A single-operator spray-injection patching unit (such as an Ausroad Jetmaster) repairs **40 to 70 potholes per day** depending on pothole diameter and travel distances between regional towns.
  * Across **~190 workable dry days per year** (accounting for wet winter days, scheduled servicing, and transit), one truck completes **7,600 to 13,300 patches annually**.
  * A fleet of **50 dedicated units** provides an annual capacity of **380,000 to 665,000 repairs** (midpoint: **522,500 patches/year**), comfortably meeting the 500,000-pothole statewide target.
* **Regional Depot Deployment:** Station trucks directly in regional state depots across Victoria's five non-metro regions (Gippsland, Hume, Loddon Mallee, Grampians, Barwon South West) rather than centralising in Melbourne, minimising deadhead transit time and maximising daily patch counts.
* **Engineering Scope Boundary:** Automated spray-patching trucks are strictly a rapid-response surface-sealing solution. Where heavy freight and water ingress have compromised the crushed-rock sub-base, patching crews will log GPS defect data to escalate the site to heavy road-rebuilding (rehabilitation) teams.

---

## 3. Bulk Procurement Strategy ("Massive Negotiation")
Individual municipalities purchase Jetpatchers one at a time at retail prices (~**$690,000–$700,000 AUD**). Placing a single state order for **50 trucks**—the largest single order in Australian history—allows Victoria to run a competitive **Best and Final Offer (BAFO)** tender under Victorian Government procurement guidelines across domestic and international manufacturers (e.g., Ausroad, Rosmech):

1. **Fleet Capital Discount (~18%):** Leverages volume manufacturing efficiencies to reduce unit price from $700,000 to **$575,000 per truck**, saving **$6,250,000** upfront.
2. **Bundled Materials, Parts & Servicing Deal (~15%):** Locks in fixed multi-year bulk supply rates for cationic bitumen emulsion, washed aggregate, spare nozzles/pumps, and preventative maintenance, reducing annual non-wage running costs from $250,000 to **$212,500 per truck** (saving **$1,875,000/year**).
3. **Local Victorian Fit-Out & Servicing Clause:** Mandates local body assembly and regional servicing hubs in Victoria (e.g., Geelong, Ballarat, or Dandenong) to guarantee rapid spare-parts turnaround and retain manufacturing jobs in the state.

## 4. Complete Financial & Staffing Maths (Budget-Neutral Model)

### 4.1 Baseline Unit Costs & Assumptions
* **1 Mid-Level VPS Admin Role:** **$145,000 / year** total employment cost (based on Victorian Public Service mid-level VPS 4–5 base salary of ~$123,000 plus ~18% employer on-costs: 12% superannuation guarantee, payroll tax, and WorkCover).
* **1 Senior Executive Service (SES) Role:** **$250,000 / year** total employment package (conservative midpoint of VPS SES-1/SES-2 bands ranging from $180,000 to $350,000+).
* **1 Jetpatcher Truck (Capital):** **$700,000** retail vs. **$575,000** bulk-negotiated (-17.86%).
* **1 Jetpatcher Truck (Annual Operating Cost):** **$500,000 / year** retail vs. **$462,500 / year** bulk-negotiated, broken down per truck as:
  * **Operator Wage + On-Costs:** **$120,000 / year**
  * **Traffic Control & Escort Vehicle:** **$130,000 / year** (required under Victorian OH&S rules for live-lane highway work)
  * **Bitumen Emulsion, Aggregate, Fuel & Servicing:** **$250,000 / year** retail -> **$212,500 / year** bulk-negotiated (-15%)

Keeping the 358-role administrative reduction permanent frees up **$28.75 million every year from Year 2 onward**—enough to fund dedicated heavy road-rebuilding crews.

## 5. Workforce Transition: Back-Office to Frontline Retraining
Reviews of the Victorian Public Service (including the Helen Silver review and 2026 government restructuring disclosures) identify **700 to 1,000 non-frontline corporate, executive, and external consultant roles** across the transport portfolio (DTP, VIDA Roads, VIDA Rail, and SRLA) that can be streamlined without affecting frontline rail or road operations.

Rather than relying purely on forced redundancies—which carry **$60,000 to $100,000 per person** in upfront statutory payout costs—this policy implements a four-step **Frontline Road Operations Transition Pathway**:

1. **Terminate External Consultants & Labour-Hire First (~150–200 roles):** End non-essential management consulting contracts (some billed at up to $1,900/hour according to CPSU disclosures) immediately, generating instant cash savings with zero redundancy payouts.
2. **Consolidate Duplicated Back-Office Units (~200–300 roles):** Merge overlapping HR, communications, media, policy, legal, and IT teams across DTP, VIDA Roads, VIDA Rail, and the Suburban Rail Loop Authority (SRLA).
3. **Voluntary Frontline Retraining & Redeployment Scheme:** Offer affected permanent back-office staff a guaranteed, state-funded transition into frontline road maintenance and oversight roles (avoiding redundancy payouts and preserving public sector jobs):
   * **Heavy Rigid (HR) Truck Licence & Jetpatcher Operator Certification (50–75 roles):** State-funded HR driver licensing, spray-injection machinery training, and regional depot placement.
   * **Traffic Management & Escort Vehicle Accreditation (50–75 roles):** Accredited traffic controller certification to operate live-lane protection vehicles alongside patching trucks.
   * **Field Road Inspectors & Contract Auditors (30–50 roles):** Retraining administrative, finance, and policy staff to inspect regional roads, log GPS pothole data, and audit private road contractors for quality compliance.
4. **Salary Maintenance & Regional Relocation Support:** Provide a 6-month wage-protection bridge for staff transitioning into lower base-band operational roles, paired with a one-off relocation grant for Melbourne-based staff moving to regional depots.

---

## 6. 12-Month Implementation Roadmap
* **Months 1–2:** Issue 50-truck BAFO tender; freeze non-essential corporate hiring; terminate non-core consulting contracts; open voluntary retraining expressions of interest for DTP/VIDA corporate staff.
* **Months 3–5:** Execute bulk supply and local fit-out contract; commence Heavy Rigid licensing, traffic control accreditation, and field inspector training cohorts.
* **Months 6–8:** Take delivery of first 25 Jetpatchers at regional depots; deploy initial public patching crews onto priority regional arterial routes.
* **Months 9–12:** Full 50-truck fleet operational statewide; launch a public online dashboard tracking potholes repaired per electorate and cost-per-patch against legacy private contracts.
