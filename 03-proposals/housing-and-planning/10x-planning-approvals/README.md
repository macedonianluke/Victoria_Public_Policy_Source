# From 300 Days to 10 Days: Planning Approvals

**A five-pillar proposal to replace Victoria's discretionary, 79-council planning maze with a rules-based, digital, single-permit system.**

> **Status:** Draft. High legal and political risk. The source is a proposal, not an audited evaluation. Every figure and legal claim must be verified before public use.

## 1. The citizen promise

**If your building fits the rules, you get a decision in days, not seasons.** A homeowner, small builder or business should not need a team of consultants and a VCAT barrister to build a compliant home, shop or warehouse.

The source proposal claims a **$4.5 billion–$7.0 billion annual economic and administrative dividend** from faster approvals, lower consultant costs, less holding interest and more supply. That is a modelled range, not an audited number. The purpose of this proposal is to test which parts are safe, legal and worth doing.

---

## 2. The problem

- **79 councils, 79 bespoke planning schemes.** The Victoria Planning Provisions were meant to create a statewide template, but local schedules, overlays and "neighbourhood character" policies have re-fragmented the system.
- **The 60-day clock is an illusion.** Requests for further information can reset the clock; post-permit "condition 1" endorsement can add months; state referral agencies operate as sequential veto points.
- **Dual permits.** A planning permit and a separate building permit re-examine the same site, plans and compliance issues.
- **Third-party appeals.** Broad VCAT appeal rights add 8–14 months and tens of thousands of dollars in legal and expert costs.
- **The cost is borne by the applicant, the buyer and the economy.** The source estimates average council processing cost at about **$4,300 per application**, applicant consultant costs at **$25,000–$120,000+**, and approval times of **140–300+ days** for standard projects, longer with appeals.

The 2025–26 reforms — the Townhouse and Low-Rise Code, the expanded VicSmart pathway and the *Planning Amendment (Better Decisions Made Faster) Act 2026* — are welcome but incremental. They do not replace 79 schemes, fuse the two permits, or end discretionary delay for routine projects.

---

## 3. The five pillars

| Pillar | What it does | Why it matters | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. One State Zoning Code** | Replace 79 local schemes with 8–10 statewide zones and objective envelope rules | Removes subjective local variation and character disputes | Heritage, environment, local democracy, transition |
| **2. Single Complying Permit** | Fuse planning and building approval into one 10-day permit for code-compliant projects | Removes double-handling and sequential delay | Certifier capacity, insurance, quality assurance |
| **3. Digital 3D pre-check (eComply)** | Mandate a statewide BIM/AI compliance engine for setbacks, height, overshadowing, parking and NCC | Catches non-compliance before lodgement; retires 79 portals | Data standards, vendor lock-in, errors, accessibility |
| **4. Parallel referrals with deemed consent** | One digital clearinghouse; state referral agencies respond within 15 business days or consent is deemed | Ends sequential vetoes and clock resets | Safety, environment, legal risk, agency capacity |
| **5. End third-party VCAT appeals on code-compliant builds** | Bar objector appeals where the project fits the code; cost orders for vexatious objections | Removes the appeal tax on compliant housing | Procedural fairness, heritage, environment, political backlash |

**The source's headline claim:** approval times fall from **140–300+ days to 10–14 business days** for compliant projects, with a **$4.5b–$7.0b** annual economic and administrative dividend.

---

## 4. What is strong, what is risky

### Strong

- The **direction** is right: rules should be objective, digital, consistent and fast.
- **Pattern-book / deemed-to-comply** approaches have precedent in Victoria and other jurisdictions.
- **Digital pre-check** can reduce errors and administrative cost if the standards are open and auditable.
- **One permit** for routine projects is a genuine simplification.
- The **economic cost of delay** is real and large.

### Risky

- **"Deemed consent" for referrals** could approve projects that harm safety, heritage or the environment. It needs exceptions, escalation and audit.
- **Abolishing third-party appeals** is a major democratic and legal change. It may require compensation, transitional justice or a narrower scope.
- **One state zoning code** could override legitimate local, environmental and cultural values. It needs co-design with councils, communities and First Peoples.
- **AI pre-check** can be wrong, opaque or captured by a vendor. It needs human review, appeals, audit and open standards.
- **Certifier capacity and insurance** are essential if private certifiers issue the single permit.
- **Council revenue and workforce** will be affected. The transition must be funded and planned.

---

## 5. A safer sequencing

Do not legislate the whole blueprint on day one. Sequence it:

1. **Pilot** in three volunteer councils and one growth corridor.
2. **Codify** a statewide pattern book and objective envelope rules for the simplest 60% of residential projects.
3. **Fuse** planning and building for code-compliant projects, with accredited certifiers, mandatory insurance and random audits.
4. **Digitise** the pre-check and referral clearinghouse, with open standards and human review.
5. **Review** the appeal settings after the pilot, with data on outcomes, objections and defects.
6. **Extend** to commercial and industrial fit-outs only after the residential pilot proves safe.

This is slower than the source's 10-day statewide vision. It is more likely to survive the Parliament, the courts and the public.

## 6. Cut / add ledger

| Cut | Add | Why it is better |
|---|---|---|
| 79 bespoke local schedules | One statewide code with local overlays for genuine values | Consistent rules, fewer consultants |
| Dual planning/building permits | One complying permit for code-compliant projects | Less double-handling |
| Sequential referrals | Parallel digital clearinghouse with clocks | Faster, accountable |
| Manual PDF checking | 3D pre-check with human audit | Fewer errors, faster |
| Broad third-party appeals on compliant builds | Upfront strategic consultation; narrow appeal rights | Less delay, more supply |
| 79 council IT portals | One state portal | Lower cost, better data |
| Discretionary "character" assessments | Objective envelope rules | Predictable, contestable |

## 8. Measures that matter

| Metric | Baseline | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median approval time, compliant projects | 140–300+ days | 10–20 business days in pilot | DTP |
| Council processing cost per application | About $4,300 | Down 50% | Councils/VAGO |
| Applicant consultant cost | $25k–$120k+ | Down 50% | Survey |
| VCAT planning appeals | To be baselined | Down for compliant projects | VCAT |
| Defect and audit rate | To be baselined | No worse than baseline | BPC |
| Housing completions | Below 80,000/year | 80,000+ | ABS/DTP |
| Community confidence | To be baselined | Stable or better | Survey |
| Heritage/environmental outcomes | To be baselined | Stable or better | DTP/DEECA |

**The one number that matters most:** Median approval time for code-compliant homes.
**The kill switch:** If defect rates rise, heritage/environmental harm increases, or community confidence collapses, stop and redesign.
