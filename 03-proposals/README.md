# Proposals

**Where a portfolio README becomes a costed, contestable, delivery-ready proposal.**

A portfolio README sets the direction. A proposal does the harder work: baseline, cost, offset, delivery model, legal pathway, workforce transition, risks, red team, and evaluation. Proposals live here so that the portfolio READMEs stay comparable.

## Status labels

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | Argument and structure are present; numbers and sources need verification. |
| **Source-checked** | Every material number is traced to a primary source and labelled. |
| **Costed** | Year 1 and Year 2+ costs, offsets and sensitivities are complete. |
| **Red-teamed** | Treasury, frontline, council/community, legal and pub-test objections have been addressed. |
| **Ready** | Ready for Cabinet, shadow cabinet or public release. |

---

## Acceptance criteria

A proposal is ready to graduate when it has:

- [ ] A one-page executive summary with the citizen promise, the cost, the offset and the first 100 days.
- [ ] A baseline grounded in primary sources, with confidence labels.
- [ ] A delivery model: make, buy, share, regulate, insure or enable.
- [ ] A cut/add ledger with named offsets and workforce transition.
- [ ] A legal and statutory pathway, including what can be done on day one and what needs legislation.
- [ ] A Commonwealth and local government interface.
- [ ] A red-team section with the strongest objections and the design mutations.
- [ ] A 12-month implementation roadmap.
- [ ] A measurement and evaluation plan with a kill switch.
- [ ] A source register and data verification appendix.
- [ ] A plain-English "pub test" explanation.

## Proposal template

Use the structure in [`../00-method/policy-readme-template.md`](../00-method/policy-readme-template.md), with additional sections:

1. **Executive summary and headline scorecard**
2. **Problem and baseline**
3. **Current machinery and statutory pathway**
4. **Delivery model and operational blueprint**
5. **Financial model and cut/add ledger**
6. **Workforce transition**
7. **Ecosystem deal and objections**
8. **Risks, red team and design mutations**
9. **12-month roadmap and 90-day pilot**
10. **Measures, evaluation and kill switch**
11. **Sources and verification appendix**

## The rule

A proposal must be able to survive four questions:

1. **The Treasurer:** "Show me the offset, the operating cost in year four and the sensitivity."
2. **The frontline worker:** "This will fail on a Tuesday afternoon because..."
3. **The council or community partner:** "You have not funded the thing you are asking us to do."
4. **The citizen who has been failed before:** "Why should I believe this time?"

If it cannot, it is still a draft.
