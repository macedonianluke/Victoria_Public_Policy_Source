# Victoria Public Policy Source

**A governing operating system for Victoria — not a filing cabinet of policies.**

This repository is the working policy library for a Victorian government-in-waiting. It is organised around outcomes a citizen can feel, the machinery that delivers them, and the cross-cutting levers that make the whole system work better. Every policy area has a README written to the same standard: the citizen promise, the current machinery, where money and time leak, the reframe, what we cut, what we add, who else must move, the first 100 days, and the measures that matter.

> **Status:** working draft for the 2026 Victorian state election cycle. Figures are sourced and date-stamped where possible, but every number still needs primary-source verification before publication. See [`04-evidence/`](04-evidence/README.md).

## The wager

Victoria does not have a policy shortage. It has a **coordination, duplication and delivery problem**.

The state spends enormous sums, employs capable people and produces good strategies. Yet the citizen experience is still fragmented: one problem, many doors; one outcome, many budgets; one place, many plans. Departments optimise for their own outputs. Agencies compete for the same capabilities. Consultants fill gaps that should be permanent public capability. Capital projects are approved one at a time, then rescued one at a time. Councils, community organisations, unions, industry and the Commonwealth all hold pieces of the same puzzle, but nobody is paid to assemble it.

The opportunity is not to do more. It is to **reconfigure what already exists**: cut the duplicated, move the displaced, price the wasteful, share the common, and reinvest the dividend in the things only government can do.

This repository is built to make that reconfiguration visible, testable and contestable.

## The operating principles

1. **Outcomes before portfolios.** A portfolio is a means. The citizen promise is the end.
2. **The citizen budget, not the agency budget.** Add up what a household actually pays and receives across taxes, rates, fares, fees, fines, concessions and services. Then design around that number.
3. **One front door, many back doors.** The citizen should not need to know the org chart to get help.
4. **Make, buy, share, regulate or insure — choose deliberately.** Every service should have an explicit delivery model with a reason, a price and an exit.
5. **Cut to invest.** No new commitment without a named offset, a sunset date or a measured return.
6. **Common platforms, local delivery.** Standardise the plumbing; diversify the front line.
7. **Buy outcomes, not hours.** Contracts should pay for results, not activity or headcount.
8. **Price the externality.** If a choice creates avoidable public cost, the price should say so.
9. **Sunset by default.** Every entity, board, program and tax concession should have to prove it still earns its place.
10. **Work with the ecosystem, not around it.** The state cannot deliver alone; it can convene, fund, regulate, insure and set the rules.
11. **Sequence for trust.** Land visible quick wins while the structural moves are being built.
12. **No orphan policy.** Every proposal names its cross-portfolio joins, its delivery partner and its evidence gap.

## The democracy and operator standard

A government that cannot show its working cannot be trusted with power. The operator standard is a cultural and legislative commitment: every significant decision is public, evidenced, owned and reviewable. The minimum standard is:

1. **Publish the baseline.** No announcement without the current number, the source and the date.
2. **Show the math.** Cost, offset, sensitivity and the operating cost in year four.
3. **Name the owner.** A minister, a department, a delivery agency and a named executive.
4. **Disclose the influence.** Who met whom, who lobbied, who donated, who advised.
5. **Open the decision record.** Problem, evidence, options, consultation, influence, cost, owner, metric, review and kill switch.
6. **Deliberate before you decide.** A real channel for the people affected, with a published response.
7. **Legislate the plumbing.** FOI, donations, truth in advertising, lobbying, appointments and local transparency.
8. **Fund the scrutiny.** Parliament, committees, audit, IBAC, the Ombudsman and OVIC.
9. **Protect the whistleblower.** Independent support, anti-retaliation and a duty to act.
10. **Report to citizens annually.** What was promised, what was delivered, what was stopped and what it cost.
11. **Sunset and review.** Every power, board, program and concession proves its value or ends.
12. **Make the operator, not the slogan, the unit of credibility.** The test is delivery, not the media release.

**The cultural test:** can the decision-maker defend the decision for three hours, unscripted, with the spreadsheet open? If not, the work is not done. The Civil-Servant Deed — 24-hour diaries, 15-day Cabinet release, open expenditure ledger, casework tracker, voting justification, frontline shifts, a seven-year lobbying ban and term limits — is how a candidate can model the standard before government.

The full argument, the cuts and adds, and the 30-plus-point legislative reform list are in [`02-levers/democracy-transparency-and-operator-culture/`](02-levers/democracy-transparency-and-operator-culture/README.md).

## The method in one page

The full method is in [`00-method/operator-method.md`](00-method/operator-method.md). In short, every policy area is examined through six lenses, adapted from Edward de Bono's parallel-thinking approach:

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
| **White — evidence** | What do we know, what is assumed, and what would change our mind? |
| **Red — trust** | How does this feel to the citizen, the frontline worker and the community? |
| **Black — risk** | What can go wrong, who is accountable, and what is the statutory constraint? |
| **Yellow — value** | What is the economic, social and fiscal return, and who captures it? |
| **Green — reframe** | What if the problem were defined differently, or the default were reversed? |
| **Blue — sequence** | What must happen first, what can run in parallel, and who owns the system? |

The green lens is where the non-linear work happens. Typical provocations:

- What if the default were public, shared or community-owned?
- What if the budget followed the citizen, not the agency?
- What if we bought the outcome and let the market organise the inputs?
- What if the state stopped doing what councils, community or the market do better — and funded them properly to do it?
- What if the asset were shared across portfolios instead of owned by one?
- What if the regulator and the operator were separated — or deliberately combined?
- What if we paid for prevention and booked the avoided cost?
- What if we merged the back office and separated the front line?
- What if we ran a reverse tender: here is the outcome, who can deliver it for less?
- What if every project had to show its operating cost for twenty years, not just its capital cost?

**The democracy test.** Every policy must also answer: who decided, on what evidence, who influenced them, what did it cost, and how can a citizen see the record and challenge it? A policy that cannot pass this test is not ready, even if the economics work.

### The "cut here, add there" ledger

Every portfolio README contains a simple ledger:

| Cut | Add | Why it is better |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicated corporate function | Shared service or common platform | Same output, lower cost, clearer accountability |
| Activity-based contract | Outcome-based contract | Pays for results, not busyness |
| Universal subsidy | Targeted entitlement + price reform | Helps the people who need it, stops paying the people who do not |
| Fragmented agency | Place-based or citizen-journey authority | One accountable owner for the whole outcome |
| Capital project with no operating plan | Whole-of-life asset budget | No more "build it and walk away" |

The rule is not "small government" or "big government". It is **concentrated government**: fewer, clearer, better-resourced institutions doing the things that only government can do, and a deliberate market, community and local government ecosystem doing the rest.

## The incoming drop zone

A human should be able to dump a loose file without knowing where it belongs. Drop it in [`incoming/drop/`](incoming/drop/), then run:

```bash
python incoming/triage.py            # dry run
python incoming/triage.py --apply    # file it, rename it, write a sidecar, update the manifest
```

The triage script classifies the file by primary policy relevance, files it in `raw/<portfolio>/`, writes a `.meta.md` sidecar and appends a line to `raw/MANIFEST.csv`. Ambiguous files go to `raw/_unsorted/` for review. The AI brief for the judgement calls is in [`incoming/TRIAGE.md`](incoming/TRIAGE.md).

## How to read a portfolio README

Every portfolio README follows the same eleven-part structure:

1. **The outcome** — the citizen promise and the measurable result.
2. **Current machinery** — ministers, departments, agencies, statute, spend and scale.
3. **Where the money and time leak** — duplication, fragmentation, failure demand, gold-plating, subsidies that reward waste.
4. **The reframe** — the non-linear move that changes the game.
5. **Cut here** — what stops, consolidates, sunsets or moves.
6. **Add here** — what is built, bought, financed, regulated or guaranteed.
7. **The ecosystem deal** — what each connected party gives and gets.
8. **Cross-portfolio joins** — the dependencies that must be managed together.
9. **First 100 days** — the visible, doable moves.
10. **Measures that matter** — outcomes, cost, equity and integrity.
11. **Open questions and sources** — what we do not know, and where the evidence is.

A detailed costed proposal lives in `03-proposals/` and links back to the relevant portfolio README. A portfolio README is deliberately high-level; it is a decision framework, not a business case.

## What "good" looks like

A policy area is ready to take to the citizenry when it can answer all eight questions:

1. **What will a household notice in the first year?**
2. **What are we stopping, and who will object?**
3. **What are we adding, and what is the offset?**
4. **Who delivers it, and what happens if they fail?**
5. **Which other portfolios must move for this to work?**
6. **How will we know it is working, and when will we stop it?**
7. **What is the source, and how confident are we?**
8. **Who influenced the decision, and where is the public record?**

If a policy cannot answer these, it is a slogan, not a plan.

## Evidence and verification

This is a living library, not a manifesto. Every figure should be traceable to a primary source: budget papers, annual reports, Auditor-General reports, legislation, ABS data, official evaluations or reputable industry data. The evidence standard is in [`00-method/evidence-standard.md`](00-method/evidence-standard.md), and the source register is in [`04-evidence/source-register.md`](04-evidence/source-register.md).

Confidence labels:

- **[A]** Primary source, current, independently auditable.
- **[B]** Official source, but dated, partial or not independently audited.
- **[C]** Stakeholder, industry or media claim; directionally useful, needs corroboration.
- **[D]** Assumption or estimate; must be tested before use.

If a number has no label, treat it as **[D]** until it is verified.

## Contributing

1. Pick a portfolio or lever.
2. Read the template and the evidence standard.
3. Copy the template into the relevant folder.
4. Keep the README high-level; put the detailed costing in `03-proposals/`.
5. Add every source to the register.
6. Red-team it: what would a sceptical Treasurer, a frontline worker, a council and a community group say?
7. Link it to at least two other portfolios or levers.

The goal is not a perfect document. It is a **contestable, coherent, delivery-ready platform** that can survive contact with the public service, the Parliament, the federation and the citizen.
